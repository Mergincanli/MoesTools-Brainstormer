# Batch R1 opening check: hesitations, gaps and unclear rules

90 hesitations, 8 gaps and 35 unclear rules in four coder reports (two coders, two units). Paths are normalised (indexes dropped).

## ra1.soviet.mammoth-tank

### Coder A: 19 hesitations, 2 gaps, 9 unclear rules

- **body.size**: chose large; alternatives huge. The Sizes rule says to pick the band whose examples name what the unit is. Large's example is 'tanks of every weight' and huge's is 'super-heavies'. The wiki prose calls the Mammoth a super-heavy tank and the manual says no tank is bigger. The rule also says a weight word in the unit's type is not a size, but it lists only light, medium and heavy, so it is unclear whether 'super-heavy' counts. I chose large because the wiki's role line says heavy assault tank, the RA1 registry notes keep it heavy, and the code's size flag (UDATA.CPP, explained in TYPE.H) puts it in one class with the Medium and Heavy Tanks. No page gives its footprint.
- **armour.class**: chose heavy; alternatives super-heavy. Step 1 is clear: armortype Heavy maps to heavy, and the registry notes list the Mammoth as staying heavy. But check A wins over every step and makes any huge unit at t3 super-heavy, so this field depends entirely on the body/size call. If size is huge, the class must be super-heavy.
- **roles.primary**: chose anti-infantry; alternatives anti-armour; assault. The rule is clear but the result looks wrong for this unit. Step 1 finds no role word in the manual paragraph. Step 4 reads official text that the unit is strong against infantry and air units, through the missiles, and the RA1 registry note confirms that text about a second weapon counts. No damage table was read, so the larger bonus can't be told, and the target named first (infantry) wins. The main weapon is an armour-piercing cannon, and the manual's 'unmatched in power on land' names no target ('land' matches no anti role). An agent who reads 'on land' as armour would choose anti-armour.
- **roles.secondary**: chose [anti-armour, anti-air]; alternatives [anti-air]; [anti-air, assault]; [assault, anti-armour]. Anti-air comes from the missiles at step 3 (official strong-against text, and they can hit high air). Anti-armour rests only on StrategyWiki's weapon heading '120mm large anti-armor cannons', counted as 'a source calls it an anti-tank weapon'. The registry says StrategyWiki is not read by step 4 or by the drop rule, but says nothing about the roles.secondary weapon tests, so another coder might ignore it and leave only anti-air. The C&C Wiki's 'effective against armor' only says what the gun is good at. Assault fits the description, but step 4 supports it only with a short- or medium-range main weapon, and the cannon bands long.
- **armament.family**: chose rockets (delivery projectile, no homing); alternatives guided-missiles with lock-on delivery. The tie-break is clear: no page read says the missiles steer or home in, so they are rockets. The source is the problem: RA1's rules.ini, which names each weapon's projectile and may make these heat-seekers, is not in EA's source release and could not be read.
- **armament.traits**: chose [anti-air, magazine]; alternatives [anti-air]. The magazine rule counts a source that names the weapon's reload as a weakness. The C&C Wiki does that: a significant reload time leaves the tank open to massed air attack. The exception for one shot per reload doesn't clearly fit a two-missile burst. But the infobox and StrategyWiki give both weapons the same cooldown, so the 'reload' may just be the rate of fire. The cannon fires the same way and doesn't get the trait only because no source names its reload.
- **armament.can_target**: chose [infantry, ground-vehicle, naval]; alternatives [infantry, ground-vehicle, structure, naval]. Step 3 or step 4. I used step 3 because StrategyWiki labels the cannons anti-armour and gives their target as Ground. Under step 3, structures count only when a source says the weapon damages buildings, and none does. Under step 4, or by the sentence that says 'ground means infantry, ground vehicles and structures', structures would be in. The rule is unclear on whether a guide's weapon heading counts as a label, and on how 'Target: Ground' interacts with the step-3 structures clause.
- **armament.can_target**: chose [infantry, ground-vehicle, structure, low-air, high-air, naval]; alternatives the same without structure. Step 4, because StrategyWiki's 'Ground and air' is not a label for only some kinds. Structures are listed because the wiki says the missiles also do well against buildings. Air is listed because sources say they attack air units and RA1 doesn't split its fliers. The anti-air trait follows from high-air.
- **armament.range**: chose long (both weapons); alternatives the same band either way. The source is unclear, not the rule. The infobox splits reach into ground and air without naming the weapons; StrategyWiki gives the shorter reach to the cannons and the longer to the missiles. Both are over 1.5 times the rifle's reach and under the tank's own sight, so long, not off-screen, either way.
- **armament.mount**: chose slow-turret; alternatives free-aim (the rockets family default). This is a proposal field. No page says where the missile racks sit. UDATA.CPP gives the secondary weapon's firing point along the turret line, so I treated it as turning with the main gun (mount tie-break step 5). Without the code, the family default free-aim would apply.
- **crew.model**: chose solo (proposal); alternatives driver-gunner. The rule is clear: TECHNO.CPP picks one weapon per target, the source never shows both firing together, and both are on the one turret. I note it only because two weapon systems on a big tank invite a gunner seat.
- **armour.models**: chose [self-repair]; alternatives self-repair plus a passive ability for the capped heal. The infobox lists the heal under 'ability', but the term self-repair says the same thing, so I used the term and no ability, as the abilities rule asks. The cap at half health has no term, so it is a fit_note. The wiki says it heals only while not taking damage, but TECHNO.CPP shows no such check, so I added an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree.
- **counters . countered_by**: chose class:vehicle and class:aircraft each appear in both lists, with reasons for different situations; alternatives vehicles only in countered_by; armour-class:heavy instead of class:vehicle for 'basic tanks'. Matchups rule 6 allows both lists when the reasons name different situations (one-on-one against outnumbered or hit-and-run; a lone aircraft against a massed air attack). It also says to place the enemy where the head-on fight at equal cost ends, and the wiki's 'basic tanks can outnumber it' could be read as the equal-cost fight. For 'basic tanks', class:vehicle is used because the Light Tank is not heavy armour.
- **weaknesses**: chose speed:medium; alternatives leave speed out of weaknesses. Rule 4 says to give the unit's own value. The wiki says no vehicle in the game moves slower, but its ratio to the Rifle Infantry is exactly 1, which is medium, so the link reads oddly next to its reason.
- **combos**: chose class:vehicle escorts and role:anti-air cover; alternatives an empty list. No source names a combo outright. Both are inferred from the wiki's warnings about being caught alone and about massed air attack. A stricter coder might leave combos empty.
- **armament.name, armament.name**: chose Twin 120mm cannons; Mammoth Tusk missiles; alternatives 120mm large anti-armor cannons; missile packs; 6-rack missile launcher. The game shows no weapon names. I took them from the manual's Weapon line and StrategyWiki's headings; the wiki's useguns field is lore.
- **sources.url, sources.kind, sources.kind**: chose The File: page URL for the manual scan; kind official for EA's GitHub source files; alternatives the static.wikia PDF URL; kind database for the source code. The RA1 registry note lists the scan as its File: page, while the RA2 and RA3 notes say to cite the PDF URL. No rule names a source kind for a publisher's source-code release.
- **tier.gate**: chose Tech Center; alternatives Soviet Tech Center. Clear. The tier_map spells it 'Tech Center' and the validator matches the exact string. The Service Depot requirement and the Allied side in the infobox are marked as belonging to the ant missions only.
- **flavour_affinity**: chose [soviet]; alternatives [soviet, allied]. Clear here. Allied scores 1 (hull-turret), or 2 if the missiles were guided missiles, which is still below Soviet's 3, so the family question above can't change this field.

Gaps (no term fitted well):

- **roles.primary**: used anti-infantry; wanted assault. The written steps make a cannon-armed super-heavy tank primary anti-infantry, because the manual's strong-against text names infantry first. Assault also can't reach the secondary list, because RA1's short rifle reach bands every tank gun long. The wiki's own role line is heavy assault tank.
- **armour.models**: used self-repair; wanted self-repair that stops at a health threshold. It heals only while at or below half health and never above it. The model can't express the cap; it is described in armour.notes.

Unclear rules:

- **Sizes: super-heavy as an example or a weight word**: Huge's example list says 'super-heavies', but the rule that a weight word in the unit's type is not a size lists only light, medium and heavy. When a wiki calls a tank 'super-heavy' and the game's code puts it in the same size class as its main battle tanks, the rule doesn't say which wins. Through check A, the answer also decides the armour class.
- **roles.secondary step 4 for assault, and the range reference**: Step 4 supports assault only with a short- or medium-range main weapon. In RA1 the reference rifle's reach is among the game's shortest, so every tank gun bands long and no RA1 tank can get assault from step 4. The registry notes even expect the tank guns to band long. This looks unintended.
- **roles.primary step 4 when official praise comes from a secondary weapon**: Official strong-against text about the unit counts even when it comes from a second weapon, and the target named first wins a tie. So a unit whose main weapon is an armour-piercing cannon becomes primary anti-infantry. The RA1 registry note explicitly endorses reading the missiles this way, but it is worth confirming that this outcome is intended.
- **Guide wording in roles.secondary and can_target step 3**: The registry bars StrategyWiki from roles step 4 and the drop rule. It doesn't say whether a guide's weapon heading ('anti-armor cannons') counts as 'a source calls it an anti-tank weapon' in roles.secondary, or as a label by targets in can_target step 3. Both of my codes depend on that reading.
- **can_target: 'ground' against the structures clause**: One sentence says a source that says 'ground' means infantry, ground vehicles and structures. Another says that under step 3 structures are listed only when the sources say the weapon damages buildings. With a 'Target: Ground' label it is unclear which applies.
- **Magazine trait and a two-shot burst**: The exception covers only a weapon that holds one shot per reload. A weapon that fires a burst of two and then waits its normal cooldown, which a wiki calls a reload and names as a weakness, meets the rule's letter. The same cooldown on the unit's other weapon then gets no trait, only because no source names it.
- **Citing the RA1 manual scan**: The RA1 registry note lists the scan by its File: page; the RA2 and RA3 notes say to cite the PDF URL. The File page's API text is only a licence template, so the copy-check never compares paraphrases against the manual itself (here the unit page's quote box covers it).
- **Source kind for EA's released source code**: The registry relies on EA's GitHub source release, but Source kinds names no kind for it. I used official as the publisher's own release; database would also be a reasonable reading.
- **Matchups rule 6 'at equal cost'**: When a wiki says basic tanks 'can outnumber' the unit, it is unclear whether that is the equal-cost fight, which would force vehicles into countered_by only, or a different situation from one-on-one, which allows both lists.
