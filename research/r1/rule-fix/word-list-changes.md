## Changed lists

### Aim / mount (`aim-models`), 1.0.0 to 1.1.0

- **Tie-break added** (`radial` / `auto-target` / `limited-arc` / `free-aim` / `slow-turret` / `body-aim`): Go down this list and take the first that fits. (1) It hits all around the unit at once, like an aura or a pulse: radial. (2) It fires by itself while the unit's player does something else (point-defence guns that fire at units, a deployed sentry gun, or launched drones that act on their own): auto-target. In a commanded game every weapon picks targets for itself, so there (2) needs one of those kinds; a turret carried on the unit (an add-on or a roof gun) goes on to the later steps, even when it picks its own targets while the unit is idle. (3) The source shows it covering only part of the circle: a gun on the side or front of a hull, a door gun, a tail gun, a mech torso that twists only partway, or a soldiers' heavy weapon that must be set up and then covers only an arc (a machine-gun team, a field gun): limited-arc. A gun that turns only a little either side of the nose (a tank destroyer) counts as fixed; see (6). If the source doesn't say how far a turret turns, treat it as a full turret. A gun the source places on the side or at the tail of a hull, or in a door, without saying how far it turns is limited-arc; one in the nose or the front of a hull is fixed; see (6). (4) A soldier or creature carries it, or it is built into a tiny or small body: free-aim. A squad's weapons all take the leader's mount. (5) It sits on a full turret, or on a torso that turns all the way round: slow-turret for the main turret (the one holding the main weapon) of a large or huge unit that moves on the ground (tracks, wheels, legs or hover), free-aim for any other turret (on a medium vehicle, a roof gun, a chin gun, a turret holding only another seat's weapon, any aircraft turret). A gun that turns with the main gun takes the same mount. When the source does not say how the main weapon is mounted, a body plan with a turret (hull-turret, multi-turret) puts it, and any weapon that takes its place, on the main turret. (6) It is fixed and fires where the unit faces (no turret, wing pods, forward launchers, bombs): body-aim. If a seat other than the driver fires it, use free-aim instead, because that seat cannot turn the unit. Game code or data that places a weapon (a firing point on the turret) counts as the source saying how it is mounted; design trivia that ties the model to a real vehicle (a side gun based on a real helicopter's) does not. The family's default mount only fills in when the source says nothing about how the weapon is mounted and the body plan gives it no turret (5).

- **Tie-break added** (`unarmoured` / `light` / `medium`): A soldier or animal is unarmoured, unless step 1 or 2 gives more, or its game data gives it tougher armour than the game's basic rifle infantry: a tougher infantry armour type or attribute, or body armour its registry notes name. Then it is light. Where the game data gives only part of its armour (one resistance, but no armour type or set), judge by that part. A power suit worn by one soldier (body size small) is light, unless step 1 or 2 gives another class (by the dow1 armour map the Space Marine Squad is light and Terminators are medium). A suit bigger than a soldier (body size medium or more) is medium. ### Cooldown bands (`cooldown-bands`), 1.0.0 to 1.1.0

- **List description:** reworded: How often an ability can be used: the wait from one use until it can be used again. Band the source's cooldown figure alone; do not add the time the effect lasts. Use real seconds at the game's normal multiplayer speed (StarCraft II: Faster). Where the source gives a span ("8 to 12 seconds", "up to 15 seconds"), band its largest value. For an ability with charges, band the time to regain one charge. Edges are exact, and a value on an edge takes the higher band. An ability whose uses come back only at a resupply point or on respawn is per-life, with or without a figure, however many uses its stock holds (a Red Alert minelayer's five mines, reloaded only at a depot, which are also its limited-ammo resource). So is an action that uses up the unit, such as a self-destruct or a spy that is lost when it enters a building. An ability with no wait has no band: leave cooldown out. That covers instant orders that can be repeated, such as unloading, and mode switches that only take time to finish; say in the summary, in words, how long the switch takes. An ability held back only by another kind of stock, such as one that refills by itself or from income (Company of Heroes munitions, the unit's energy), has no band unless the source also gives a cooldown. A boost's own fuel tank, which only that ability burns and which refills by itself, is its cooldown, not a stock: band the time it takes to refill from empty. An energy pool stays a stock even on a unit with only one ability that spends it (the StarCraft II Medivac's Heal).
- **Changed `per-life`** (Per life):
  - label: Once per life -> Per life
  - description: reworded: Uses that come back only at a resupply point or on respawn, however many the stock holds (often one per life or purchase), or an action that uses up the unit.

### Roles (`roles`), 1.0.0 to 1.1.0

- **List description:** reworded: What the unit is for: its job or the targets it is best against, never how it does it (mechanics are patterns). A card has one primary role and up to two secondary roles. Requires says what a unit needs before it can claim a role. A source unit never claims a role whose requires it fails. If such a role still fits, leave it out and record it in fit_notes (path roles, wanted: that role). Meeting the requires is not enough: the role description must fit too. Official text means words the game or its makers wrote: the in-game role line, tooltip or production text, the manual, the official unit profile and patch notes. It still counts when read through a page that copies it. A wiki's own words (its infobox labels, prose and tables), strategy guides and internal file names are not official text; the registry notes say which infobox fields copy in-game text. For a unit recorded once for several sides, official text counts only when every side's version carries it. Official words name a role only through the role's whole label or one of its aliases, in any word form (harasses for harasser, scouting for scout, Infiltration for Infiltrator), and only when they describe the unit's own job; each half of a slash label (Artillery / siege) counts as a label. One word of a longer label is not enough: 'Assault' alone does not name Frontline assault, so a Purpose such as 'Tank/Structure Assault' names no role. The unit's own name counts only when it names the unit's job (Engineer, Spy, Tank Destroyer, Slave Miner). These words name no role: main battle tank; light, medium or heavy tank; core or line infantry; multipurpose; general-purpose; attack (as in attack helicopter); support when it means firepower (combat support, fire support, artillery support, close air support); control in mind control or a control point; and aura in a healing or repair aura (that job is support). Words that would give a role do not count when they hedge whether the unit or weapon does that job at all (to some extent, up to a point, can serve as); words that hedge only how much it destroys (potentially, at times) still count. How to pick the primary and secondary roles is under roles in docs/fields.md. The tie-breaks settle close calls.
- **Changed `assault`** (Frontline assault):
  - source_rule: reworded: As primary: any generalist that is not fast with light or no armour (step 5 of roles.primary), or the closest fit for a unit that no earlier step reaches (step 6). As secondary: a main weapon that reaches no further than the main guns of the game's main battle tanks counts as short to medium range here (roles.secondary step 4).
- **Changed `raider`** (Raider / flanker):
  - source_rule: reworded: Needs a weapon that is not a tool (roles.primary step 2): a unit that harms nothing cannot hit and run. As primary: a fast or very fast generalist with light or no armour (step 5 of roles.primary). As secondary: see roles.secondary.

### Sizes (`sizes`), 1.0.0 to 1.1.0

- **List description:** reworded: Footprint seen from above, which sets the hitbox. Pick the band whose examples name what the unit is. Examples win over measurements, because strategy games draw soldiers oversized. A weight word in the unit's type (light, medium, heavy or super-heavy tank or mech) is not a size. Count the hull, fuselage and wings, not a rotor disc or a gun barrel. Only for a unit that no example covers, measure. Compare it with the same game's foot soldier: one soldier is small, two to three soldier-widths is medium. When it is wider than that, or the game has no foot soldiers, compare it with the game's main battle tank: clearly smaller is medium, about the same is large, twice or more is huge. Where no source gives the footprint of a unit that no example covers, these stand in for it, in this order: the vehicle whose hull or chassis it is built on, whose size it takes; then the game's own size data, set against the same data for its main battle tank, such as a size group in the code or rules (the tank's group is about the same, a smaller group clearly smaller) or the room it takes in a transport (twice the tank's room or more is twice or more).
- **Changed `medium`** (Medium):
  - description: reworded: About two to three soldier-widths: buggies, cars, trucks, armoured cars, APCs and IFVs, light mechs and light walkers, light scout helicopters.
- **Changed `large`** (Large):
  - description: reworded: Tanks of every weight (light, medium and heavy tanks, tank destroyers, self-propelled guns), carriers built on a tank hull, harvesters (ore trucks included) and mobile refineries, medium to assault mechs, attack and transport helicopters, jets, submarines.
- **Changed `huge`** (Huge):
  - description: reworded: Two tanks or more: tanks at least twice a battle tank's size, giant mechs, airships.

### Weapon traits (`weapon-traits`), 1.0.0 to 1.1.0

- **Changed `magazine`** (Magazine):
  - source_rule: reworded: Set it only when the sources show the reload as part of how this weapon fights: they describe it firing a burst or salvo and then stopping to reload, name its reload or clip as a strength or weakness of this weapon, or say its ammo runs out and comes back on its own. A reload time in a stats table, a passing mention, or a comparison of how often two guns reload does not count. A weapon that holds one shot per reload (a single-shot launcher or cannon) never gets it for that reload: its reload is its rate of fire, so say in the summary, in words, how long it takes. The same holds for a fixed burst that the game repeats at its normal rate of fire (a pair of shells or missiles, then a wait), even when a source calls the wait a reload: only a clip or an ammunition count in the sources (so many shots, then a reload of their own) makes it a magazine.
- **Changed `root`** (Root / pin):
  - source_rule: added: Set it on any weapon whose sources say it stops the target moving for a time, whatever its family (a stasis ray, a magnetic lift, a freeze). When they say the target can neither move nor fire, set root and silence together. A stop of only a moment is stun. No family or damage type brings it by itself: a cryo weapon gets it only when its sources say the frozen target cannot move.
- **Changed `silence`** (Silence / disarm):
  - source_rule: added: Set it on any weapon whose sources say it stops the target firing or using abilities for a time, whatever its family; with root when the target can neither move nor fire (see root). No family or damage type brings it by itself.
