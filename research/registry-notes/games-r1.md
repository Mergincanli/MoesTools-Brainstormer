# Games registry, batch r1: notes

Entries written: `ra1` (Red Alert with Counterstrike and The Aftermath) and `ra2` (Red Alert 2 with Yuri's Revenge), in `games_r1.json`. A suggested replacement for `ra3` is in `games_r1_ra3_suggestion.json`. All three pass `check_games.py`.

## Choices that need a look

1. **No faction tag on tier gates that more than one faction id uses.** The validator accepts a unit's gate only if the gate has no faction or exactly the unit's faction. Both games have `shared` units, and RA2 has country subfactions (for example `ra2.iraq.desolator` needs the Radar Tower). A `soviet` tag on Radar Tower would reject the Desolator. So shared gates (Barracks, War Factory, Radar Dome, Battle Lab and so on) carry no tag; only gates that a single faction id uses are tagged (RA1 Kennel and Tesla Coil; RA2 Psychic Radar, Grinder and Robot Control Center). Suggested validator change: also accept a gate tagged with the parent of the unit's faction. Then the tags could go back on.
2. **Which gate to record.** T1 gates are the production buildings. For T2 and T3, record the highest tech building in the unit's chain. RA1 aircraft come from the Helipad or Airfield, which need a Radar Dome, so they use the gate "Radar Dome". RA2 Harriers and Black Eagles use "Airforce Command Headquarters", which both builds them and is the tech step.
3. **Service Depot is T2** in both games. It is one building beyond the basics, and the wiki's war factory and barracks pages list its units (minelayers, MCVs, the RA1 Field Mechanic) under tier 2. **The RA1 Kennel is T1.** It is the dogs' own production building and needs only the barracks; the wiki lists the dog as tier 1.
4. **Stolen tech is T3** (RA2: Chrono Commando, Chrono Ivan, Psi Commando, and Yuri Prime in RA2 only). It needs a Spy inside an enemy Battle Lab, and the Allied Spy itself needs a Battle Lab.
5. **RA2 subfactions.** The nine countries are listed as subfactions with a parent, because each fields at most one unit no other country has: the Sniper (Great Britain), Tank Destroyer (Germany), Black Eagle (Korea), Terrorist (Cuba), Desolator (Iraq), Demolition Truck (Libya) and Tesla Tank (Russia). The USA (a paradrop) and France (the Grand Cannon, a defence) have no unique unit, but they are listed so the set is complete. **RA1 countries** (England, France, Germany, Greece, Spain, Turkey; Russia, Ukraine) only change stats and have no unique units, so they are left out.
6. **`shared`.** RA1: Rifle Infantry, Rocket Soldier, Engineer, Ore Truck, MCV, Transport, Tanya (multiplayer) and the Aftermath Demolition Truck. RA2: the Engineer (all three sides in Yuri's Revenge) and the Amphibious Transport. The transport has a separate version per side, and its armour differs (light for the Allies, heavy for the Soviets and Yuri), so it can be recorded per side instead. The RA2 Attack Dog belongs to the Allies and Soviets but not Yuri, so it is not `shared` in Yuri's Revenge. Record it under one side and name the other in its notes, because the schema has no "two of three sides" faction.
7. **Flavour.** Yuri gets no `flavour_affinity`. Psionics matches one Empire theme, but the faction as a whole (mind control, cloning, grinders, Gattling guns, saucers) does not clearly share any one flavour's spirit. The countries carry their parent's affinity.
8. **Five sources on `ra2`, one over the 1-4 guide.** The anchor uses three tanks with different prices, so it needs three price pages, plus one page each for tiers and speed. If four is a hard cap, drop the Lasher source and keep "Lasher (700)" in the basis.

## Speed

- **The reference is Speed 4 in both games:** RA1's Rifle Infantry and RA2's Conscript (the GI and Initiate are also 4). The wiki's land, sea and air speeds repeat the rules Speed. Both engines turn Speed into a step of Speed × 256 / 100 leptons per tick, with fractions dropped and a cap of 255; a cell is 256 leptons. So Speed 4 is a 10-lepton step.
- **The step-1 rule "ratio = land speed / 4" holds only for RA2 walkers, drivers, ships and Fly aircraft.** RA1 bands compare paces worked out from the released code, as set out below. This replaces the step-1 examples (RA1 Medium Tank 2.0, Mammoth 1.0) and the first fix's factors (× 0.8 × 16/15 and the rest).
- **Round once.** speed_raw is the exact ratio rounded to two decimals (fields.md). Never round a ratio that was already printed to three decimals: that slip gave 1.65 for 1.64496 and 4.32 for 4.3148 in an earlier draft.
- **Where the Research notes below say otherwise, this section wins for speed.** Three of their lines are amended to match: the RA1 engine line (take speed from the rules file), the UnitBox line (band by this section, not by dividing by 4) and the line on sources that would not load (a reference copy of the rules now loads). The RA1 Figures paragraph and the RA2 chrono line are amended too.

### Red Alert 1: bands from the code

**Sources.** The code is EA's release in the Remastered Collection:
- [TECHNO.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/TECHNO.CPP)
- [INFANTRY.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/INFANTRY.CPP)
- [COORD.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/COORD.CPP)
- [DRIVE.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/DRIVE.CPP)
- [UDATA.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/UDATA.CPP)
- [UNIT.H](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/UNIT.H) and [VESSEL.H](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/VESSEL.H)
- [FLY.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/FLY.CPP) and [AIRCRAFT.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/AIRCRAFT.CPP)

EA's 2025 release of the original 1990s source has the same routines: the same line-for-line `While_Moving`, `Movement_AI` and flight code ([CODE/DRIVE.CPP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Red_Alert/main/CODE/DRIVE.CPP)). Only its 8.8 fixed-point maths differs, and that gives the same throttles. The ground rates and the Tracked flags come from a [reference copy of Rules.ini](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRA/raclassic/master/ref/Rules.ini) and its [aftrmath.ini](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRA/raclassic/master/ref/aftrmath.ini).

**Checks.** Two independent tick-by-tick models agreed to two decimals on every ratio; they differ only in the third decimal of a few diagonals. A third check of the per-cell arithmetic, which reads the track tables from DRIVE.CPP and the sine table from COORD.CPP, matched them. The formulas below give the same numbers without simulating.

- **Which figure.** Take Speed and the Tracked flag from the rules file: Rules.ini, with aftrmath.ini on top (the game reads them in that order).
  - The wiki infobox repeats Speed for every unit checked except two: the Rocket Soldier (rules 3, wiki 4, StrategyWiki 3) and the Mobile Gap Generator (rules 9, wiki 4, StrategyWiki 9).
  - For those two, use the rules figure and add an uncertain entry with sources_disagree.
  - This overrides the advice under Research notes, Red Alert 1, Figures, to band from the wiki infobox. By the lead's call the reference copy now counts for every rules value, not only speed: where it and a wiki disagree, the copy wins, with an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree.
- **m** is Speed × 256 / 100 with fractions dropped: 3 → 7, 4 → 10, 5 → 12, 6 → 15, 7 → 17, 8 → 20, 9 → 23, 10 → 25, 12 → 30, 14 → 35, 16 → 40, 20 → 51, 40 → 102.
- **The reference pace.**
  - A walking soldier sets full throttle on every leg. Only formation moves read the ground table, so the clear-ground Foot 90% never slows it.
  - It steps m leptons a tick toward the next cell's spot, snaps onto the spot once within 16 leptons, and spends the next tick starting the following leg without moving.
  - The step also rounds down: one lepton short going north or east, full going south or west (COORD.CPP).
  - So a straight cell takes floor(240 / (m − 1)) + 3 ticks going north or east, and floor(240 / m) + 3 going south or west.
  - For the rifleman that is 29 and 27 ticks, or 8.83 and 9.48 leptons a tick. Its straight pace is **9.155** (the four axis directions averaged; 9.15453 unrounded). Its diagonal pace is **9.25**: a diagonal cell is 362 leptons, three diagonals take 38 ticks, and north-east takes 43.
  - Divide every straight pace by 9.155 and every diagonal pace by 9.25.
- **Infantry** follow the same rule. Ratios are straight, with the diagonal in brackets:
  - Speed 3 (Rocket Soldier, Flamethrower Infantry, Shock Trooper): 0.70, slow (0.71).
  - Speed 4: 1.00.
  - Speed 5 (Grenadier, Tanya, civilians): 1.19 (1.23).
  - The Attack Dog doubles its step whenever it has a target (m = 20): 1.86, fast (1.93). Nothing limits this, and it is how the dog moves in a fight, so the band counts it. On a plain move order the dog walks at 1.00; say so in mobility.notes.
- **Vehicles.**
  - Tracked=yes takes the Track rate and anything else the Wheel rate (UDATA.CPP). The wheeled vehicles are the Ranger, MCV, Mobile Gap Generator, Supply Truck and the Aftermath Demolition Truck.
  - Read the flag from the reference copy of the rules. Batch entries whose mobility/base uncertain note says the rules file could not be read, or that no page names the drive (M.A.D. Tank, Mobile Gap Generator, Minelayer, Mammoth), should now cite it. For the M.A.D. Tank the band turns on it: wheeled, it would be very slow (0.47).
  - As a vehicle sets off into each cell, its throttle becomes the clear-ground rate × 255: 204 tracked, 153 wheeled.
  - Each tick it banks a = m × throttle / 256, rounded to the nearest whole number. Each 10 points buy one step along a track table.
  - The step that closes a cell drops whatever is left over, and the next cell starts in the same tick.
  - So a straight cell (256 leptons) takes floor(240 / a) ticks, and a diagonal cell (362 leptons) takes floor(320 / a). The direction makes no difference.
  - Tracked, straight ratio with the diagonal in brackets: Speed 3 0.70 (0.74), 4 0.93 (0.98), 5 1.17 (1.22), 6 1.40 (1.51), 7 1.64 (1.78), 8 1.86 (1.96), 9 2.15 (2.30), 10 2.33 (2.45).
  - Wheeled, the same way: Speed 6 1.08 (1.12), 8 1.40 (1.51), 9 1.64 (1.78), 10 1.75 (1.86).
  - Worked example, Mammoth Tank: m 10, a = 10 × 204 / 256 = 7.97 → 8. A straight cell takes 240 / 8 = 30 ticks, so 8.53 leptons a tick: 0.93, slow. A diagonal cell takes 40 ticks, so 9.05: 0.98, also slow.
  - Worked example, Ranger: m 25, a = 25 × 153 / 256 = 14.9 → 15. A straight cell takes 16 ticks, so 16.0 leptons a tick: 1.75, fast.
  - Worked example, Mobile Gap Generator: m 23, a = 23 × 153 / 256 = 13.7 → 14. A straight cell takes 17 ticks, so 15.06 leptons a tick: 1.64496, stored 1.64, fast. The Heavy Tank and V2 (Speed 7, tracked) bank the same 14 and get the same ratio.
- **Ships.** They use the vehicle routine on open water at full throttle (Float 100%, so 255), with one more step that the code takes without meaning to.
  - The routine halves the top speed of any unit carrying a capture-the-flag flag. Unlike the flag test in the teleport code, this one does not check that the object is a land vehicle first.
  - Read through a ship, the flag field is the low byte of the word that holds two repair flags. VESSEL.H puts that word right after the type pointer, where UNIT.H keeps the flag's owner. The heap zeroes it.
  - So the byte reads as house 0 to 3 and never as 'no house', and every ship moves at half rate: a = floor(m / 2) × 255 / 256, rounded.
  - The same holds in both builds. The original makefile packs structures byte by byte and does not force int-sized enums, so the house type is one byte there ([CODE/MAKEFILE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Red_Alert/main/CODE/MAKEFILE)). The Remastered code declares it as a char. A small layout test compiled with GCC, packed and unpacked, reads the byte as 0.
  - This is an inference from the code and the class layouts. No play test or community source confirms it.
  - At half rate, straight with the diagonal in brackets: Speed 4 (Cruiser) 0.58, very slow (0.61); 5 (Missile Sub) 0.70, slow (0.74); 6 (Destroyer, Submarine) 0.82, slow (0.87); 9 (Gunboat) 1.33, medium (1.35); 14 (Transport) 2.00, fast (2.17).
  - At full rate they would be 1.17, 1.40 (1.51), 1.75, 2.80 and 4.66.
  - Worked example, Submarine: m 15 is halved to 7, so a is 7. A straight cell takes floor(240 / 7) = 34 ticks, so 7.53 leptons a tick: 0.82, slow.
- **Aircraft and helicopters** cruise at full throttle with no per-cell loss.
  - Helicopters keep full throttle until three cells from the target. Fliers move in whole pixels (tens of leptons) and carry the leftover from tick to tick (FLY.CPP).
  - The straight pace is about m − 0.5, and the diagonal a little lower. The Spy Plane, with the largest step, loses a little more to rounding: about 100.75 for its m of 102. So the ratio is the pace over 9.155:
    - Speed 12 (Hind, Chinook): 3.22.
    - Speed 16 (Yak, Longbow, Badger): 4.31.
    - Speed 20 (MiG): 5.52.
    - Speed 40 (Spy Plane): about 11.0.
  - All of them are very fast.
- **Which lens.** Store the straight ratio (the four axis directions averaged) as speed_raw.
  - If the straight and diagonal ratios fall in different bands, take the band nearer medium, store the ratio that lies inside it, and add an uncertain entry. Ore Truck, Artillery and Demolition Truck (1.40 and 1.51) are therefore medium.
  - Add an uncertain entry too when a ratio rounds onto an edge. The M.A.D. Tank, the Speed-3 infantry and the Missile Sub all sit at 0.70, which is slow.
  - A second model that ran mixed-heading paths agreed. The Rocket Soldier dipped just under 0.7 there, and the edge rule keeps it slow.
- **Left out of bands:**
  - vehicles and ships at half health or less, which slow to three quarters;
  - prone soldiers, who crawl at half speed (civilians run at double);
  - land vehicles carrying a flag;
  - Ukraine's faster ground units, difficulty settings and crates;
  - formation moves, the opening turn and crowding.

### Red Alert 2 with Yuri's Revenge

- **No per-cell check is possible.** RA2's code is not released. The method assumes that a walker and a driver with the same Speed keep the same pace on open ground, because both turn Speed into leptons per frame the same way ([ModEnc, Speed](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/Speed/On_Unit_Types); [Phobos docs](https://phobos.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Fixed-or-Improved-Logics.html)). That is an inference.
- **The stored ratio is on the Speed scale** (the figures as the sources give them, divided by 4). Check whole leptons as well: the step (Speed × 256 / 100, fractions dropped) times the ground's share, against the Conscript's 10. At full share (walkers, Drive, Ship and Fly units) this changes no stock unit's band. Checked against every unit in Yuri's Revenge's rulesmd.ini, the one unit it moves is the Navy SEAL, under the amphibious share.
- **Walking infantry: land speed / 4.** Desolator 4 is 1.0; Boris 5 is 1.25 (1.2 in whole leptons).
  - ModEnc's two-thirds and three-halves infantry factors are prone speeds; Phobos made them editable as prone settings and calls the vanilla values hard-coded.
  - Never apply them to a walking soldier or to the reference.
- **Amphibious infantry** (SpeedType Amphibious: Tanya, Navy SEAL, Yuri Prime) cross clear ground at 80% of Speed ([ModEnc, LandTypes](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/LandTypes)). Take land speed × 0.8 / 4:
  - Tanya 6 (Yuri's Revenge): 1.2, medium.
  - Yuri Prime 6: 1.2, medium. In whole leptons both are 15 × 0.8 = 12 against 10, the same.
  - SEAL 5: exactly 1.0 on the Speed scale, on the edge between slow and medium. In whole leptons it steps 12 × 0.8 = 9.6 a frame, or 9 if that product is truncated too, against the Conscript's 10: 0.90 to 0.96, slow. With no released code, which comes first cannot be told, so it takes medium by the edge rule, with speed_raw 1.0 and an uncertain entry that names the lepton reading. If the lead reads the lepton premise strictly, it is slow at 0.96 instead.
- **Drive vehicles and Ship units: land or sea speed / 4.** Clear ground costs tracks, wheels and floats nothing. Examples:
  - Grizzly 7: 1.75, fast.
  - Rhino 6: 1.5, medium.
  - IFV and Terror Drone 10: 2.5, fast.
  - Chaos Drone 8: 2.0, fast.
  - Mastermind 4: 1.0.
  - Magnetron 5: 1.25.
  - Slave Miner 3: 0.75, slow (0.70 in whole leptons, on the edge, also slow).
  - The Chrono Miner drives and teleports only back to a refinery ([ModEnc, Locomotor](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/Locomotor)), so band it as a Drive vehicle.
- **Fly aircraft** (Harrier, Black Eagle): air speed / 4. Both are 14, so 3.5, very fast.
- **Hover units** (the Robot Tank and the Allied, Soviet and Yuri amphibious transports) get no speed_raw.
  - Clear ground holds them to half their Speed (ModEnc LandTypes), and a global boost raises it on straight runs ([ModEnc, HoverBoost](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/HoverBoost)).
  - ModEnc's Locomotor page puts hover units roughly a third slower than drive units with the same Speed.
  - No source shows how these combine. So judge between half and three quarters of land speed / 4, take the band nearer medium when that range crosses an edge, and add an uncertain entry.
  - Robot Tank 10: 1.25 to 1.9, medium, leaning fast.
  - Transports 6: 0.75 to 1.1, medium.
  - In whole leptons the low ends are 1.2 and 0.7, which changes neither band.
- **Jumpjet fliers** (Rocketeer, Siege Chopper, NightHawk Transport, Kirov, Floating Disc) fly on their rules JumpjetSpeed, not on the Speed the wiki shows ([ModEnc, JumpjetSpeed](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/JumpjetSpeed); ModEnc's [Speed page for JumpjetControls](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/Speed/In_JumpjetControls) says Red Alert 2's jumpjet units use it in place of Speed).
  - Yuri's Revenge's rulesmd.ini gives 30 for the Rocketeer, Siege Chopper and NightHawk, 5 for the Kirov and 16 for the Floating Disc. Their wiki air speeds are 9, 12, 14, 5 and 15.
  - No source says what unit the value is in, so give no speed_raw. Band from the possible readings (÷ 10 as leptons per frame, or ÷ 4 on the Speed scale) and the wiki's comparisons, and add an uncertain entry.
  - The Rocketeer, Siege Chopper and NightHawk are very fast on every reading (3.0 or 7.5). Do not band the Rocketeer from its wiki air speed, which would give fast.
  - The Kirov and the Floating Disc depart from the letter of the comparison rule in speed-bands.json, which with no usable figure gives a compared unit's band. Each one's mobility/speed uncertain entry must say so:
    - **Kirov: slow.** The wiki calls it the slowest Soviet vehicle. Read literally, that hands it the band of the slowest others (the Apocalypse, V3, War Miner and MCV at 1.0: medium, which on an airship would need the atypical entry `mobility:airship allowed.speed-bands medium`). This text departs from that: those vehicles sit exactly on medium's lower edge, so anything slower is under 1.0. Its readings are 0.5 (÷ 10) or 1.25 (÷ 4, which the wiki's comparison rules out), so the range left runs from 0.5 to just under 1.0, straddles 0.7, and takes slow by the edge rule (very slow if ÷ 10 is right).
    - **Floating Disc: fast.** The wiki says only that it outpaces the Kirov. Read literally, that gives it the Kirov's band, slow. This text departs from that, because outpacing a slow unit sets only a floor. Its readings, 1.6 or 4.0, straddle 2.5, so it takes fast by the edge rule.
- **Teleporting infantry** (Chrono Legionnaire, Chrono Commando, Chrono Ivan) never walk.
  - A move order jumps them to the spot at once. They then stay frozen while they phase in.
  - The freeze depends on the distance and on game-wide settings, not on their Speed: distance divided by a factor, never under a minimum ([ModEnc, ChronoDistanceFactor](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/ChronoDistanceFactor); [ModEnc, ChronoMinimumDelay](https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/ChronoMinimumDelay)). The rules file calls the Legionnaire's Speed a dummy value.
  - Yuri's Revenge sets the factor at 48 leptons a frame and the minimum at 16 frames. Over a jump plus its freeze, that gives about 1.6 times the Conscript's pace for a one-cell hop, 3.2 times for two cells, and 4.8 times from three cells on.
  - Band them very fast, with no speed_raw and an uncertain entry. The foot mobility type allows only slow to fast, so the coder needs the atypical entry `mobility:foot allowed.speed-bands very-fast`.
  - The line under Research notes, Red Alert 2, saying that their infobox land speed sets the phase-in time should be reworded.
- **Damage.** Damaged Drive and Ship units slow to three quarters (ModEnc Speed). Leave it off.
- **Where the rules values come from.** The jumpjet and chrono values come from the YRinis.zip archive that [the wiki's Rules.ini page](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Rules.ini) links. The archive is http only, so it cannot be a sources URL. It predates patch 1.001's price changes, and 1.001 changed no speeds.

## Damage and armour maps (not in `sources` because of the 1-4 guide)

- **RA1 damage:** EA's released source code lists the nine warheads (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/RULES.CPP), and DEFINES.H in the same folder says what each is for. SA (small arms) and HollowPoint (sniper bullets) map to kinetic, HE and Nuke to explosive, AP to armour-piercing, Fire to fire, Super (the Tesla warhead) to electric, Organic (the dog's bite) to melee, and Mechanical (the Field Mechanic's vehicle repair) to repair. The Medic's heal uses Organic too (the reference copy of Rules.ini gives its Heal weapon that warhead); record any healing weapon as repair, whatever its warhead.
- **RA2 armour:** from the ModEnc page https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/Armor_types (11 types). none and flak map to unarmoured: flak is a slightly tougher soldier type, and the wiki gives it to the Conscript. plate (armoured elite infantry) maps to light, as do light and special_1 (the Terror Drone). medium maps to heavy: ModEnc says only miners and the Kirov use it, and the Kirov should still be recorded as super-heavy (a T3 airship). heavy maps to heavy. Left out: wood, steel and concrete (buildings and defences) and special_2 (missiles in flight).
- **No RA2 damage map.** Each RA2 weapon names its own warhead in the rules, dozens of them, not a short fixed list. Use step 5 under armament[].damage.
- Wiki text sometimes disagrees with the infobox. The Grizzly's text says medium armour and the Lasher's says light, but both infoboxes say Heavy. Trust the infobox, which reflects the game data.

## Uncertain

- **RA1 Tesla Coil:** the manual says it needs a Tech Center, but in the game it needs only the War Factory (the wiki notes this). We follow the game, so the Tesla Tank and Shock Trooper are T2.
- **RA3 Soviet Naval Yard:** its infobox says tier 2, but its own production list puts its basic boats at tier 1 with no extra gate. The suggestion maps it to t1.
- RA1's last Westwood patch, 3.03, was a beta that was never officially released, but the wiki treats it as the final version. The Remastered Collection's balance patches mostly touched Tiberian Dawn and RA1's low-power rules. They also removed Aftermath units from Quickmatch, but those units stay in skirmish.

## RA3 suggestion (`games_r1_ra3_suggestion.json`)

- **Adds missing production and tech gates.** Soviet: Barracks t1, Naval Yard t1. Allied: Boot Camp t1, Seaport t1, Airbase t1, Defense Bureau t3 (Uprising FutureTech units such as the Cryo Legionnaire, Pacifier FAV, Future Tank X-1 and Harbinger Gunship). Empire: Instant Dojo t1, Dojo Upgrade t2, Dojo Breakthrough t3, Imperial Docks t1, Docks Upgrade t2, Docks Breakthrough t3. Without these, an agent researching a Conscript, Peacekeeper, Shinobi or any ship has no matching gate. Soviet aircraft use "Super Reactor", because the Airfield needs one, and the Kirov uses "Battle Lab". The Uprising Grinder needs a Super Reactor and a Crusher Crane (a tier 1 building), so its gate is "Super Reactor".

- **Roles step 1 reads the paragraph.** Units have no designation (only buildings get a Purpose line), so the paragraph is the unit's official one-line description.
  - It names a role only through a role label or alias that describes the unit's own job, in any word form. Examples: the Ranger and the Gunboat are good at scouting (scout); the APC and the Transport Helicopter transport infantry (transport); the MiG fights hit and run (raider); the Field Mechanic repairs vehicles (support).

- **Roles step 4 and the secondary drop rule read the paragraph too.** There are no separate strong-against or weak-against lines.
  - Strong: the paragraph says the unit is best or effective against a kind of target, or that it destroys, quickly damages, devastates or mows through it. Text about the unit counts even when it is about a second weapon: the Mammoth's missiles make it strong against infantry and aircraft.
  - Weak: the unit is an easy target for a kind of target (the Field Mechanic, for infantry and tanks). Read it for the roles.secondary rule that drops a role.
  - Either counts only when it names a kind of target. Tanks, armored units, targets or craft, and vehicles mean armour; infantry and troops mean infantry; aircraft, air units and airborne attackers mean air.
  - These match none of the three anti roles: a named unit (the Yak as prey for Rocket Soldiers), fast-moving targets (V2), sea defence (Cruiser), land or ground targets, structures, buildings, bases, ships and submarines.
  - A qualifier such as "in groups" keeps the target (Rifle Infantry, Grenadier). When one sentence names two targets, the one named first wins a tie.
- **No step 4 bonus from the game data.** The infoboxes give each weapon's warhead (SA, HE, AP and so on), and the reference copy of Rules.ini gives the warheads' damage table: each warhead's Verses against the armour types none, wood (buildings), light, heavy and concrete, with aftrmath.ini adding the Mechanical warhead. No warhead does more than full damage to any type, only full damage at most to some and less to the rest, which roles step 4 does not count as a bonus. So step 4 still rests on the manual's strong-against text, or on a main weapon that can hit nothing else.
- **Shared units.** Every RA1 unit is one design with one rules entry, whichever side builds it.
  - A paragraph the manual prints once is official text for the shared entry, even when it sits only under Allied units (Rocket Soldier, Tanya).
  - Where the manual prints a unit under both sides with different paragraphs, only what both carry counts. For Rifle Infantry that is infantry; tanks appear only in the Allied paragraph. The Engineer, Ore Truck, MCV and Transport paragraphs say the same on both sides.
- **The manual's side is not the faction.** Take the faction from the infobox. For example, the MRJ is printed under Soviet units but is Allied in skirmish (https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Radar_jammer).
- **The stat words are neither role text nor band data.** Range and Armor are the manual's own grades and often clash with the game's types (Light Tank, MCV, Gunboat and Submarine, among others; the Field Mechanic's None matches the rules copy but not his infobox). Bands and the armour class use the reference copy of the rules, which the infobox mostly repeats (see Armour class in the research notes). The Weapon word is only a name.
- **Not official.**
  - The UnitBox `role` field (for example Main battle tank), its `useguns` and `usearmor` fields (the wiki's lore), the one-line `desc` under each unit in the production buildings' lists, and the prose.
  - StrategyWiki's RA1 unit pages (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Command_%26_Conquer:_Red_Alert/Allied_units and https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Command_%26_Conquer:_Red_Alert/Soviet_units) end each unit with Strong vs. lines and short ability lines. ## Game-wide mechanics

Mechanics that belong to the game, not the unit (see Shared mechanics and universal keys in the Conventions). Leave them off every unit's weapons and abilities. A unit that does one of them better or worse than the rest says so in its strengths or weaknesses.

### Red Alert 1 (`ra1`)

Written in batch R1 preparation, before any RA1 unit is coded, from the Red Alert and Aftermath manual scans, the Command & Conquer Wiki pages linked below and EA's released Red Alert source code. Code files named in capitals (UNIT.CPP and so on) are at https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electronicarts/CnC_Remastered_Collection/master/REDALERT/<FILE>.

- **Basic orders** (move, attack, stop, guard, force fire, force move, scatter, escort, teams and formations; the Remastered Collection adds guard area, Ctrl+Alt on a spot): every unit. No pattern. RA1 has no stances, attack-move, retreat or reinforce order ([manual scan](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/File:Red_Alert_1_Manual.pdf); [Remastered guide](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Command_%26_Conquer:_Remastered_Collection_manual)). Exceptions go in notes, never in abilities:
  - Guard sends an aircraft home to its pad.
  - Aircraft never escort. Unarmed units (the manual names the Engineer and the Medic) cannot be force-fired and do not escort.
  - Tanya has no guard order: she fires only at targets the player picks (manual).

  The Remastered Deploy key also unloads carriers, lays mines and starts the M.A.D. Tank and the Chrono Tank ([patch 735514](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Remastered_Collection_patch_735514)). It is only a key binding: record each of those actions on its own unit.
- **No veterancy:** RA1 units never rank up ([Veterancy](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Veterancy) starts with Tiberian Sun). No `earn-veterancy`, and no ability has source veterancy.
- **No garrisons:** no RA1 building takes infantry in to fight ([Garrisoning](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Garrisoning)). Infantry enter buildings only through one-shot actions of their own (Engineer, Spy, Thief, Tanya's C4); record each on its unit.
- **Transports** (`carry-passengers` on the carrier, never on the riders):
  - The APC and the Transport Helicopter each carry five infantry. The naval Transport carries five ground units and loads and unloads only at a shore (manual; [Transport](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Transport)).
  - Entering and leaving are universal keys. Each carrier keeps one passive for its seats (Paired entries). No page shows riders firing out, so the seats are passenger-cargo.
  - When the APC dies, its infantry riders climb out if there is room (UNIT.CPP): on_death bail-out, basis in-game.
  - A destroyed aircraft or ship takes its riders with it (AIRCRAFT.CPP, VESSEL.CPP): say so in on_death.notes.
  - Riders die when the APC is sent through the Chronosphere (DRIVE.CPP; the rules key ChronoKillCargo is on by default in RULES.CPP): say so in crew.notes.
- **Crew survivors** (on_death, not an ability):
  - A ground vehicle with the rules flag Crewed and no passenger seats has an even chance, when it dies, of letting one crewman out with part of his health: a Rifle Infantry if the vehicle is armed, a civilian technician if not (UNIT.CPP).
  - A Crewed aircraft drops a Rifle Infantry by parachute nine times in ten, but only over ground infantry can stand on (AIRCRAFT.CPP).
  - Infantry and ships never leave a crewman: the code has no survivor logic for them.

  Which units are Crewed is set in the rules file, and the reference copy (Rules.ini with aftrmath.ini on top, OpenRA's raclassic repository) shows it. Crewed=yes: the V2 Rocket Launcher, the Light, Medium, Heavy and Mammoth Tanks, Radar Jammer, Mobile Gap Generator, Artillery, Ore Truck, MCV, Ranger, both minelayers, Yak, Longbow, Hind and the Aftermath Tesla Tank. No survivor (Crewed=no, or no key, which the code reads as no): the M.A.D. Tank, Chrono Tank, Demolition Truck, Phase Transport, Supply Truck, MiG and Transport Helicopter; ships are never crewed (VDATA.CPP), and the APC's riders get out instead (Transports). A unit the copy marks Crewed=yes, together with the code that lets a crewman out (UNIT.CPP for ground vehicles, AIRCRAFT.CPP for aircraft), counts as a cited page showing this unit's crew getting out: code bail-out with basis in-game, and cite the copy and the code file, or name them in an uncertain entry when the unit already has five sources. Otherwise code what a cited page shows, or the decision-3C default (bail-out, basis proposal). Either way, say in on_death.notes that the crewman gets out only by chance: an even chance for a ground vehicle, nine times in ten for an aircraft over ground a soldier can stand on.
- **Repair, rearming and healing at buildings** (no pattern; leave all of these off):
  - The Service Depot repairs vehicles for credits and reloads minelayers ([Service depot](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Service_depot_(Red_Alert_1))); the code also lets an aircraft land on it for repair (AIRCRAFT.CPP).
  - The Helipad reloads helicopters and the Airfield reloads planes ([Helipad](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Helipad_(Red_Alert)), [Airfield](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Airfield_(Red_Alert_1))).
  - The Naval Yard and the Sub Pen repair docked ships (manual).

  A unit that repairs or heals others records it on itself (the Medic and the Field Mechanic: `repair-allies`). Self-repair is per unit too: the Mammoth Tank and the Ore Truck regain health by themselves, but only up to half (TECHNO.CPP; [Mammoth tank](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Mammoth_tank_(Red_Alert_1)), [Ore truck](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Ore_truck)). Code the armour model self-repair on those two.
- **Ammunition** (resource `limited-ammo`, not an ability): every armed aircraft carries a fixed load and must fly home to rearm ([Helipad](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Helipad_(Red_Alert)), [Airfield](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Airfield_(Red_Alert_1)), [Longbow](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Longbow_(Red_Alert_1))). Code limited-ammo on each armed aircraft and leave the trip home off. Each Airfield holds one plane, and a plane whose Airfield is gone has nowhere to land (manual): a weakness of the planes. Minelayers work the same way: five mines, refilled only at a Service Depot (manual).
- **Aircraft in flight take half damage** (AIRCRAFT.CPP). No pattern and no armour model: mention it in armour.notes of every aircraft.
- **Crushing** (mobility trait `crush`, not an ability): the code fixes which vehicles run over infantry (UDATA.CPP); no rules key changes it.
  - Crushers: the V2, the Light, Medium, Heavy and Mammoth Tanks, APC, Ore Truck, MCV, both minelayers, Radar Jammer, Mobile Gap Generator, Tesla Tank, Chrono Tank, M.A.D. Tank and Phase Transport.
  - Not crushers: the Ranger, the Artillery and the Demolition Truck.
  - Infantry can be crushed, except the Shock Trooper (Aftermath manual; [Shock trooper](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Shock_trooper_(Aftermath))).

  The player orders a crush with force move. Tracked vehicles also roll over sandbags and barbed wire, and vehicles never crush each other (manual). Use the trait only where its size rule allows.
- **Going prone** (no pattern, not an armour model): infantry other than dogs drop flat when frightened and standing still, and a prone soldier takes half damage (INFANTRY.CPP; ProneDamage in RULES.CPP). Mention it in armour.notes where it matters. Never code damage-reduction or evasive for it.
- **Damaged vehicles and ships slow down** (no pattern): at half health or less they move at three quarters of their speed (DRIVE.CPP; ships share that code, VESSEL.H). Leave it off; speed bands use full health.
- **Crates** (a multiplayer option; picking one up is a universal key): ground and naval units collect them, submerged submarines too; aircraft cannot ([Crate](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Crate)). Leave every crate effect off (money, free units, boosts, healing, map reveal, blasts, one-shot powers, invulnerability, cloaking). A unit cloaked only by a crate gets no cloak.
- **Side-wide powers** (left off every unit; none gives named unit types a new button, so no RA1 ability has source commander-power):
  - GPS Satellite (Allied Tech Center): reveals the map and gives radar (`reveal-area`; [GPS Satellite](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/GPS_Satellite)).
  - Chronosphere (Allied): moves one friendly vehicle, ship or soldier for a while (`teleport`; [Chronosphere](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Chronosphere_(Red_Alert_1))). Infantry sent through die, a Demolition Truck blows up, and a ship sent to land or a vehicle sent to sea is lost. The naval Transport, aircraft and the Chrono Tank cannot be sent (HOUSE.CPP). Each use can also set off a time quake or a chrono vortex.
  - Iron Curtain (Soviet): makes one vehicle, ship, aircraft or building immune to damage for a while, and a Demolition Truck only for a moment (`become-invulnerable`; HOUSE.CPP). The [Iron Curtain](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Iron_Curtain_(Red_Alert_1)) page says it kills riders in transports and cannot cover buildings; the code shows neither, so add an uncertain entry with sources_disagree where it matters. The M.A.D. Tank cannot detonate under it (UNIT.CPP; [M.A.D. tank](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/M.A.D._tank)), and Tanya's C4 does nothing to a building under it (INFANTRY.CPP).
  - Atom Bomb from the Missile Silo ([Nuclear missile silo](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Nuclear_missile_silo_(Red_Alert_1))).
  - The Airfield's Paratroopers, five Rifle Infantry dropped by a Badger (`drop-in-from-above`; [Paratroopers](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Paratroopers_(Red_Alert_1))), and its Spy Plane ([Spy plane](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Spy_plane_(Red_Alert_1))).
  - Parabombs are a campaign-only Airfield power ([Parabombs](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Parabombs)). In skirmish they come only from a crate, or as a one-shot that a Spy earns inside an enemy Airfield.
  - The one-shot Sonar Pulse that a Spy earns inside an enemy Sub Pen ([Sonar pulse](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Sonar_pulse)).

  The Badger and the Spy Plane are not buildable units, so do not research them. What a Spy earns by getting inside belongs to the Spy. A unit that a power treats differently says so in its notes.
- **Country bonuses** (side-wide, numbers only, a tenth each): England tougher, Germany harder-hitting, France faster-firing, Ukraine faster, Russia (USSR in the Remastered Collection) cheaper; the Remastered-only Greece longer range, Spain infantry with more armour, damage and speed, Turkey none now. Leave them off: bands use the stock unit with no country, and cost_band.raw is the list price.
- **Sight, shroud and radar** (no pattern): every unit clears the shroud around it, and the host may let the shroud grow back ([Fog of war](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Fog_of_war); manual). The radar map needs a powered Radar Dome or the GPS.
  - Aircraft have sight 0 in the game data. In flight they reveal nothing; a landed aircraft reveals one cell (AIRCRAFT.CPP). Band their sight from the 0, basis in-game.
  - The wiki's Helipad and Airfield lists say aircraft in flight never show on the radar map. That is game-wide and not the sensor trait stealth: mention it in sensors.notes.
- **Cloaking and detection** (per unit, apart from one engine default):
  - The Submarine and the Missile Submarine stay hidden underwater and must surface to fire (manual; [Submarine](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Submarine_(Red_Alert_1))). Code both the same way.
  - The Spy's disguise belongs to the Spy (`disguise`).
  - Detectors the sources name get the sensor trait detector. The Attack Dog is the only unit that sees through Spies (manual; INFANTRY.CPP). The Gunboat, Destroyer and both submarines find submerged units ([Stealth](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Stealth), [Destroyer](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Destroyer_(Red_Alert_1)) and the Naval Yard and Submarine Pen pages).
  - By engine default, every infantry type and every ship makes a cloaked enemy next to it shimmer into view (IDATA.CPP, VDATA.CPP, FOOT.CPP). That is game-wide and gives no unit the detector trait.
- **Capturing buildings** (`capture-structure`): not game-wide. Only the Engineer captures enemy buildings ([Engineer](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Engineer_(Red_Alert_1)); manual). It captures a building whose health is in the red; otherwise it damages it, never far enough to destroy it, and the Engineer is used up either way (INFANTRY.CPP). Say so in the Engineer's ability summary.
- **Game-mode and economy rules** (leave off): skirmish has no capture points. Capture the Flag is a multiplayer option, and only vehicles carry the flag (UNIT.CPP). Vehicles can be sold at a Service Depot; infantry and ships cannot (manual).

### Red Alert 2 with Yuri's Revenge (`ra2`)

Written in the batch R1 preparation, before any `ra2` unit is coded, for Yuri's Revenge patch 1.001. Sources: the two manuals (PDF links under Official role lines), the Command & Conquer Wiki pages named below (https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/<Title>, read through the API) and ModEnc (https://modenc.renegadeprojects.com/<Page>).

- **Basic orders** (no pattern): every unit. Move, attack, stop, guard (an area, a structure, or escorting a unit), attack-move, scatter (soldiers step out of a vehicle's path), force-fire, force-move (which also drives a vehicle over a unit), waypoints and patrol loops, teams and type select (YR manual, Basic and Advanced Keyboard Functions; RA2 manual). Neither manual has a retreat or reinforce order.
  - The deploy key is not an order. Where it does something on a unit (GI sandbags, Desolator radiation, Siege Chopper landing, Yuri Clone psychic wave), it is that unit's ability. On a transport or a garrisoned building it lets the riders out, which is the universal evict key.
- **Veterancy** (earn-veterancy): every unit that fights becomes Veteran, then Elite, by destroying enemies worth more than three times its own price (Veterancy page). Two other routes are game-wide too and stay off: a veterancy crate promotes every unit, friend or foe, in a square of 3x3 cells around it (Crate page), and a Spy that gets into a hostile Barracks or War Factory makes the infantry or vehicles its owner builds afterwards start Veteran, aircraft and paradrops included (Spy page).
  - Veteran gives one fixed lift to damage, speed, armour and firing rate. Elite keeps that lift without adding to it, and adds self-healing, automatic scattering and, on most units, a better weapon (Veterancy page; ModEnc EliteAbilities and VeteranAbilities). Rank healing gives no self-repair model and no ability.
  - Elite weapons (Paired entries): one that only reaches further or hits harder goes in the stock weapon's summary. One that changes family, damage type or traits is an acquired weapon with Elite rank as its condition, linking earn-veterancy: the elite Tesla Trooper's bolt that jumps to more targets, the cannon an elite Slave Miner gets in place of its gun.
  - Units that cannot rank up say so in weaknesses: the Robot Tank (its page) and the Aircraft Carrier, whose Hornets take the experience, though a crate can still promote it (its page). The Engineer can hold a rank but gains nothing from it (its page). ModEnc Trainable says a Spy in a factory overrides the no-rank setting, so a Robot Tank built there may still start Veteran; nobody has tested it, so add an uncertain entry if it matters. An empty elite line in an infobox only hints that a unit cannot rank up: check its page.
  - Hornets, Ospreys and Slaves have a tiny internal price, so a single kill makes them Elite (Osprey and Slave pages).
  - Riders: infantry in an IFV are never promoted, because the IFV does the firing (Veterancy page). In Yuri's Revenge, infantry garrisoned in a structure rank up while inside (YR manual, Gameplay Changes item 10). For Battle Fortress riders the sources disagree: the Veterancy page says they rank up, the Battle Fortress page says the Fortress takes the credit. Add an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree.
- **Garrisons** (carry-passengers, recorded on the building or bunker, not on the riders): only basic infantry enter civilian buildings: the GI, the Conscript and the Initiate. The Guardian GI cannot (YR manual; Garrisoning page).
  - Occupants fire a different, stronger weapon from inside; infantry infoboxes give it on its own garrison line. It comes with the shared mechanic, so leave it out of the rider's armament and mention it in notes or strengths.
  - Built garrisons: the Soviet Battle Bunker (five basic infantry), Yuri's Tank Bunker (one turreted vehicle, any but the Magnetron) and Yuri's Bio Reactor (infantry inside raise its power). A weapon that clears garrisons is recorded on the unit that carries it.
- **Transports** (universal keys): boarding, unloading and leaving a building are universal keys, on every transport too. Each carrier keeps one passive for its seats (Paired entries): passenger-sets-weapon for the IFV, whose rider sets its weapon, and for the Battle Fortress, whose five riders fire their own weapons from its ports with extra reach and damage (its infobox); carry-passengers for the Flak Track, the NightHawk Transport and the Amphibious Transport. The Grinder, Bio Reactor and Tech Hospital are buildings, not transports.
  - Riders when the carrier dies: the Battle Fortress page says they walk out unhurt, so its on_death is bail-out, basis in-game. No other transport page read says what happens to riders; write nothing about them unless the unit's own page does.
- **Crew survivors** (no pattern): the engine can let one crewman escape a destroyed vehicle (ModEnc Crew System), but no source read gives Red Alert 2's chance or says which vehicles have crews. So a crewed vehicle takes the 3C default (bail-out, basis proposal) unless its own page shows survivors.
- **Repair and healing at buildings** (no pattern): leave all of these off.
  - Allied and Soviet Service Depots repair ground vehicles and the Amphibious Transport for credits, and pull out Terror Drones (Allied Service Depot page; RA2 manual). A depot cannot fix the Siege Chopper (its page).
  - Naval yards repair ships, and so does Yuri's Submarine Pen (RA2 and YR manuals; Submarine pen page). Yuri has no building that repairs land vehicles or aircraft; the Grinder recycles units and repairs nothing (Grinder page).
  - The Airforce Command Headquarters repairs and rearms its Harriers or Black Eagles, four per building (its page).
  - Captured tech buildings (Tech building page): a Tech Outpost works as a Service Depot; a Tech Hospital heals infantry, and in Yuri's Revenge all of the owner's infantry wherever they are; a Machine Shop repairs all of the owner's vehicles (the YR manual says vehicles; the Tech machine shop page adds ships and aircraft).
  - Terror Drones inside a vehicle are removed by a depot, a Tech Outpost, an IFV carrying an Engineer, the Iron Curtain and the heal crate (Allied Service Depot, Iron Curtain and Crate pages). Use them in the Terror Drone's countered_by and the IFV's strengths.
  - A unit that repairs others records it, such as the IFV's Engineer weapon. A unit that mends itself without a rank has the self-repair armour model: the Apocalypse Tank, Chrono Miner, Giant Squid, Mastermind and the mobile Slave Miner (their infoboxes).
- **Crates** (picking one up is a universal key): leave crates off, because no crate gives a unit a weapon or a button. Any unit can open one, submerged units included. A crate can give money, a veterancy rank, an armour, firepower or speed bonus for nearby units, a full heal for everything the player owns, a free ground vehicle or a view of the whole map (Crate page).
- **Side-wide powers and buildings**: leave them off every unit. None gives named unit types a new button, so no ability takes source commander-power (RA2 and YR manuals).
  - Superweapons: Nuclear Missile, Iron Curtain, Weather Control Device, Chronosphere, Genetic Mutator, Psychic Dominator. Support powers: the Tech Airport's paradrop, America's paradrop, the Soviet Spy Plane, Yuri's Psychic Reveal and every side's Force Shield. Buildings: Spy Satellite Uplink, Gap Generator, Psychic Radar, Industrial Plant, Ore Purifier, Cloning Vats, Grinder.
  - Mention an effect in notes where it matters for a matchup: the Iron Curtain makes vehicles untouchable but kills infantry; the Chronosphere moves vehicles, kills soldiers it moves (riders inside a moved transport live), and wrecks ships it puts on land or tanks it puts in water; the Genetic Mutator turns infantry into Brutes and kills dogs and sea creatures; the Psychic Dominator takes lasting control of every unit that is neither immune nor garrisoned (their pages).
  - Boris's MiG strike and Yuri Prime's control of buildings are those units' own innate abilities, not powers.
- **Mind control** (mind-control, on the units that do it: Yuri Clone, Yuri Prime, Mastermind, Psi Commando). Any unit can be taken except those the sources call immune: miners, Attack Dogs, Masterminds, Yuri Clones, heroes and every flier (YR manual, Yuri Clone entry), and the Robot Tank, Terror Drone, Chaos Drone, Brute and Slaves (their pages). A controlled unit goes back to its owner when its controller dies (Mind control page); the Dominator's control lasts.
  - Being controllable needs no entry. An immune unit says so in strengths, and a matchup with a mind-control unit links `pattern:mind-control`.
- **Heroes** (Yuri's Revenge; availability unique): Tanya, Boris and Yuri Prime. The YR manual allows one hero at a time (Gameplay Changes item 9); the Tanya and Yuri Prime pages add a free copy for each Cloning Vat. Say so in notes.
  - Heroes cannot be mind-controlled, and no vehicle but the Battle Fortress can run them over (item 8).
  - The YR manual says heroes heal on their own, but their infoboxes list healing only at Elite. Follow the infobox (no self-repair model; rank healing is veterancy) and add an uncertain entry at armour/models, marked sources_disagree, that cites the manual.
- **Submerged units** (cloak). The Typhoon, Boomer, Dolphin and Giant Squid cannot be seen or shown on radar while under water. The Typhoon comes up only when shot at or when a ship comes near; the Boomer comes up to fire missiles at land targets or when damaged; the Giant Squid shows itself while it attacks (their pages).
  - Code mobility naval-submerged and one passive ability, source innate, linking cloak. Give no stealth trait: stealth still lets enemies see the unit up close, and fully invisible is the cloak pattern (Sensor traits).
  - Only the Destroyer and its Osprey, and the submerged units themselves, find them (Typhoon and Stealth pages). Give those units the detector trait.
- **Disguise and spy detection.** The Spy and the Mirage Tank pass as something else: disguise, recorded on those units.
  - Give the detector trait only where a page or infobox says the unit sees through a Spy: the Attack Dog, the Yuri Clone and the Psi Commando. Not Yuri Prime: his page says he cannot. YR Gameplay Changes item 20 says every psychic unit and structure can; where no page confirms it (the Initiate, the Mastermind), give no trait and add an uncertain entry at sensors/traits, marked sources_disagree, that cites item 20.
  - The NightHawk Transport only stays off enemy radar; eyes still see it (RA2 manual; its page). The stealth trait claims more, so give it no trait and add a fit_note at sensors/traits.
- **Shroud and sight** (no pattern): skirmish and multiplayer have no fog of war (ModEnc FogOfWar; RA2 manual, The Shroud). Black shroud covers unexplored ground and a unit lifts it within its Sight. Lifted ground stays in view, enemy units on it included, until a Gap Generator or a Spy in a radar building restores the shroud.
  - The minimap comes from each side's radar building while it has power, so no unit has the radar trait.
  - Every unit's sight band is medium, basis proposal (registry sight_reference). Put its Sight figure in sensors.notes. Scout cannot rest on long sight.
  - Off-screen range: its first reason compares the reach with the figure the sight band uses, and a proposal band uses none. So a weapon is off-screen only by the rule's other two reasons (a source says it needs spotters or fires beyond its own sight, or the shot is steered through its own camera). Otherwise far-reaching artillery stays long.
- **Crushing** (the mobility trait crush, not an ability): vehicles the game makes crushers run over crushable soldiers, and force-move sends them over on purpose. In Yuri's Revenge the Battle Fortress also flattens vehicles, even tanks, and walls (YR manual). Give crush only to vehicles whose sources show it and whose size the trait allows (ModEnc Who crushes whom and OmniCrusher).
  - Soldiers the sources call uncrushable say so in strengths: the Tesla Trooper, Brute, Crazy Ivan, Chrono Legionnaire, the three stolen-tech commandos, the deployed Guardian GI and the heroes (their infoboxes).
- **Prone infantry** (no pattern, no armour model): soldiers drop flat when shot at, and warheads with a prone setting then hurt them less. A GI in sandbags does not count as prone (ModEnc ProneDamage; GI page). Mention it in armour.notes only where a source makes it matter.
- **Radiation** (not game-wide): Desolator fields, the nuclear missile and the fallout of a destroyed Nuclear Reactor kill infantry and hurt light vehicles (RA2 manual). The weapon that leaves it gets the zone trait. Units the sources call immune say so in strengths: the Terror Drone and Chaos Drone (their infoboxes), the Desolator (RA2 manual) and the Slave Miner, though not its Slaves (its page).
- **Base power** (no pattern): low power switches off radar and many defences (RA2 manual). The Robot Tank stops working when its Robot Control Center is lost or runs short of power (YR manual; Robot Tank page). That is the Robot Tank's own weakness, recorded on it, not a game-wide mechanic.
- **Engineers, capture and infiltration**: not game-wide. Record capture-structure on the units that do it: the Engineer, which is used up on each building it takes (RA2 manual; Engineer page); the Spy, which works on buildings from inside; and Yuri Prime, who takes buildings by mind control (YR manual).
- **Aircraft rearming** (no pattern): Harriers and Black Eagles fly back to an Airforce Command pad to reload after an attack run, and Hornets fly back to their carrier (Airforce Command Headquarters and Hornet pages). Reloading is a base feature. A unit whose every weapon runs dry gets the limited-ammo resource and no finite-ammo trait (finite-ammo's coding rule).

### Red Alert 3 (`ra3`)

Written for pilot round 1, then checked and extended to Uprising's skirmish units in the R1 batch preparation. Pages named here are on the Command & Conquer Wiki (https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/<Title>); the manuals are the two PDFs named under Official role lines.

- **Basic orders** (move, attack, stop, stances): every unit. No pattern. The other orders in the manuals' command lists (attack-move, reverse and formation moves, force-fire, scatter, waypoints and the like) and the four stances stay off too. The F key fires the unit's own special ability. That is not a basic order, so record it on the unit.
- **Veterancy** (earn-veterancy): every unit climbs to Veteran, Elite and Heroic by fighting, by picking up veterancy crates, or by being built while its side holds Veteran Academies. The Cryocopter and the Cryo Legionnaire never rank up (their infoboxes, and the Multigunner IFV page); those units say so in weaknesses. Infantry riding in a Multigunner IFV are not promoted while inside, since the car does the firing (Veterancy page).
  - **More exceptions.** The Sputnik's and the Nanocore's infoboxes say they cannot rank up; the Sputnik says so in weaknesses. The Allied and Imperial Engineers do not rank up on their own, but a Veteran Academy still promotes them (Tech buildings (Red Alert 3) page). They need no weakness for it.
  - **What ranks add stays with veterancy:** more damage and toughness (the Veterancy page and the manuals differ on which rank adds what) and, at Heroic, faster attacks (both manuals) and more range, sight and speed, immunity to bribes, healing out of combat and, for infantry, immunity to the bark and roar (Veterancy page). None of this is an armour model, a sensor or an ability, and bands use the unranked unit. A weapon whose damage category changes at a rank keeps the unranked category, with the change in its summary (the Apocalypse Tank's cannons turn Explosive at Heroic). The Threat Meter, which speeds up promotion while a side fights hard, stays off too.
  - **Stock self-repair.** Five units heal out of combat before they rank up: the Apocalypse Tank, Kirov Airship, FutureTank X-1, Harbinger Gunship and Giga-Fortress (Veterancy page and their infoboxes). Code the self-repair armour model on them. The faster healing at Heroic stays with veterancy.
- **Garrison** (carry-passengers, recorded on the building or bunker, not on the riders): infantry can enter garrisonable buildings and battle bunkers. Attack dogs and war bears cannot.
  - **Tesla Troopers.** Both manuals also name Tesla Troopers as unable to garrison, while the Tesla trooper page lists voice lines under a garrisoning heading (they may belong to charging a Tesla Coil). Follow the manuals: a weakness, plus an uncertain entry with sources_disagree.
  - **Range bonus.** Garrisoned infantry fire further (patch 1.11 made the bonus smaller). It stays off, and range bands use the soldier in the open.
  - **Clearing garrisons** belongs to the unit's own weapon or ability: the Conscript's Molotovs and the Desolator's Deathspray (Garrisoning page), the Mortar Cycle's Molotovs (Barracks (Red Alert 3) page), the Peacekeeper's riot shield (Boot camp (Red Alert 3) page) and the Imperial Warrior's Banzai charge (Instant dojo page). Peacekeepers and Imperial Warriors lose one of their own for each occupant they kill (Garrisoning page). The Desolator Airstrike is a protocol and stays off.
- **Enter, leave and evict passengers**: universal keys, on every transport too. A transport keeps one passive for its passenger seats (see Paired entries).
  - **Evacuate**, the special ability of the Riptide ACV and the Twinblade (their infoboxes), is the evict key and stays off.
  - **Which pattern.** Only the Multigunner IFV changes its weapon with its rider (passenger-sets-weapon). The Bullfrog, Riptide ACV, Twinblade, Sudden Transport and Century Bomber have cargo seats (carry-passengers). The Twinblade can lift one vehicle instead of its infantry (Twinblade page and profile); that is part of its seat passive, not a button.
  - **Buttons that do more than unload** are recorded on the unit: the Bullfrog's man-cannon and the Century Bomber's paradrop.
- **Top Secret Protocols** (side-wide powers bought with security points): a protocol that changes every unit of a class stays off, such as Terror Drone Surprise, which can make a destroyed Soviet vehicle release a Terror Drone. A protocol that gives named unit types a new button or effect is recorded on each of them, with source commander-power. A protocol that only changes numbers is not an ability. High Technology is one: it shortens ability cooldowns (aircraft carrier, attack dog, Cryocopter, Guardian tank, Harbinger gunship) and makes some effects stronger or longer (the dog's bark, the Guardian's target painter, the carrier's blackout missile, the Cryocopter's beams, Cryo Legionnaire damage). Name it in the summary of the ability it changes, or for weapon damage in that weapon's summary, never as an ability of its own.
  - **None gives a new button.** No protocol on the Top Secret Protocol page gives a named unit type a new button. Strikes, call-ins and economy protocols (Free Trade, Cash Bounty, Mass Production, Robotic Assembly and the like) belong to no unit and stay off.
  - **Class-wide number changes stay off.** Bands use the stock unit, and the numbers go in the notes of the field they change: Advanced Aeronautics (every Allied aircraft gets more health, sight and ammunition, and reloads faster), Fortified Fleet (the Yari, Sea-Wing, Naginata and Shogun get tougher, faster and see further), Advanced Rocket Pods (rocket weapons fire more rockets), Grinder Treads (Soviet vehicles regain health when they crush, and the Grinder when it hits), and the area powers Point-Defense Drones and Emperor's Rage.
  - **Death effects go in notes.** Honorable Discharge makes Imperial vehicles blow up when destroyed. Terror Drone Surprise gives a destroyed Soviet vehicle or ship a chance to release a drone. Name either in on_death.notes, never as an outcome.
  - **Number changes to named abilities.** Honorable Discharge also makes the Yari's Last Voyage and the Burst Drone's self-destruct blasts bigger. Name that in those abilities' summaries, as for High Technology.
  - **Call-in units are not researched:** the Artemis bomber (Surgical Strike), the Sky Knights of Surveillance Sweep, the Final Squadron drones, the Sleeper Ambush Tankbusters and the Badger bombers. Badgers come from the Desolator Airstrike protocol and from Natasha's airstrike (Barracks (Red Alert 3) page), which is her own ability.
- **Repair and rearming** (no pattern):
  - **Repair drones.** The Allied Armor Facility, Airbase and Seaport, the Imperial Mecha Bay and Imperial Docks, and the Soviet Crusher Crane send repair drones that mend nearby vehicles, aircraft and ships, one drone to a unit (Repair drone (Red Alert 3) page). The drones also pull Terror Drones out of infested units (same page), and destroy Burst Drones latched onto vehicles (Burst Drone page). The Soviet War Factory, Naval Yard and Airfield repair nothing (their pages).
  - **Other healing.** Health crates, the Heroic rank, and the Hospital, Garage, Dry Dock and Airport tech buildings, which heal their owner's infantry, ground vehicles, ships and aircraft once captured (Tech buildings (Red Alert 3) page), heal units too. Leave all of this off, and never count it as a unit's own self-repair.
  - **Structures** are repaired by selecting them and pressing C (both manuals). Engineers can also repair structures (Barracks (Red Alert 3), Boot camp (Red Alert 3) and Instant dojo pages): record that on each engineer. Units that heal others record that on themselves, such as the Allied Engineer's medic tent.
  - **Rearming.** Aircraft with a set load (the Vindicator, Apollo Fighter, MiG Fighter and Century Bomber) reload at an airfield. The reloading stays off.
  - **Return to Base.** The Return to Base button of the Vindicator, Apollo and MiG (Return to Base page) is their own special ability. Record it, linking retreat-to-base.
- **Crates** (opening one is the universal pick-up key): some maps hold credit, health and veterancy crates. The health and veterancy crates also affect the units around the one that opens them (Crate page). Leave them off.
- **Armour facing** (armour model directional, not an ability): the Uprising manual says tanks and other armoured vehicles take more damage from the side, and more still from behind.
  - **Units whose infobox gives figures** all show the same small rise: a little more from the side and a little more again from the rear. Eighteen do: the Hammer, Guardian, Tsunami, Apocalypse, Mirage, King Oni, V4, Sickle, Bullfrog, Sudden Transport, Soviet MCV, Soviet ore collector and Sputnik, Uprising's Grinder, Mortar Cycle, Pacifier and Reaper, and the Assault Destroyer. Code directional on them, and say in armour.notes that the difference is small.
  - **Other tanks and armoured machines.** A vehicle, hovercraft or walker of armour class medium or heavier whose page gives no figures (such as the FutureTank X-1) is directional by the manual's rule. Add an uncertain entry: its page gives no figures, and the manual makes the rear sound much weaker than any infobox figure does. Pages with the same armour set differ here (the Apocalypse lists figures, the FutureTank X-1 none), so a missing figure is a gap in the wiki.
  - **Leave it off** a light vehicle whose page gives no figures, with an uncertain entry saying so (as the Multigunner IFV sample has), and off drones, infantry, aircraft, and ships whose infobox gives no figures.
- **Crushing** (the mobility trait crush, not an ability): heavy vehicles run over smaller units (the Hammer tank profile, the Grinder page), and force move or crush move sends a vehicle to do it on purpose; that order is basic. A unit runs over another when its crush level is above the other's crushable level (FutureTank X-1 page). About half the infoboxes give the levels: basic infantry, engineers, the Tankbuster and the Archer Maiden are crushable at 10; the Tesla Trooper, Cryo Legionnaire and Desolator at 20; Natasha and Yuriko at 50; the three main battle tanks crush at 20 and are crushable at 20. Code the trait from the unit's own page, with the levels as support, where the trait's class and size rules allow. Grinder Treads stays off.
- **Taking over an empty vehicle** (recover-wreck): when Natasha snipes a vehicle's pilot, the vehicle turns neutral and infantry can then claim it (Natasha Volkova page). Leave the claiming off the infantry; Natasha records her snipe.
- **Capturing buildings** (capture-structure): not game-wide. Only the Combat Engineer, the Allied Engineer and the Imperial Engineer capture (Barracks (Red Alert 3), Boot camp (Red Alert 3) and Instant dojo pages): record it on each of them. They also take neutral tech buildings such as Oil Derricks, Observation Posts and Veteran Academies (Tech buildings (Red Alert 3) page). A neutral building is not capture-structure (Pattern links), so name that in the capture ability's summary. What such a building gives its owner (income, a wide view, healing, ranks for new units) is side-wide and stays off. A Spy or Shinobi getting into a building is its own ability.
- **Stealth and detection**: not game-wide. Code each unit's hiding and spotting on the unit, with the cloak, burrow or disguise patterns and the stealth and detector sensor traits (Stealth page).
  - **Hiders:** the Akula, Dolphin, Sea-Wing and Yari hide by submerging, and each also spots other submerged units. The Mirage Tank, Spy and Sudden Transport hide by disguise.
  - **Detectors:** the Attack Dog, War Bear and Burst Drone see through the disguises of Spies, Mirage Tanks and Sudden Transports (Stealth page; the Uprising manual says so for spies).
  - **Off the units:** the Surveillance Sweep protocol also reveals hidden units, and it stays off. The Hold Fire stance, which the manuals suggest for hidden units, is a basic order.
- **Sight and radar** (no pattern): what every unit sees adds to its side's view (the Fog of War section in both manuals), and construction yards give the side radar (Allied construction yard (Red Alert 3) page). A unit that sees further or spots hidden enemies says so in sensors.
- **No retreat or reinforce order**: neither manual's command list has one.

## Research notes for the r1 agents

- **Best source:** the Command & Conquer Wiki, read through its API. The production building pages (barracks, war factory, shipyard, airfield) list units under tier headings with their extra requirements, and each unit's infobox gives cost, land speed, armour type, tier and internal name. Space requests out and retry after a 429.
- **For RA2 rules meanings** (armour types, Verses, warhead flags), use ModEnc (modenc.renegadeprojects.com). Its pages download directly with `?action=raw`.
- **For RA1 engine behaviour,** EA's GitHub source release shows what the code does. Numbers such as cost live in RULES.INI. The wiki's infoboxes repeat most of them, but the reference copy of the rules file (OpenRA's raclassic repository; Rules.ini, then aftrmath.ini on top) is the source for every rules value: where the two disagree, the copy wins, with an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree, as the Speed section does for speed. A key the copy leaves out keeps the code's built-in default (RULES.CPP and the type tables, such as VDATA.CPP, which makes every ship a scanner and never crewed).
- **Research what is buildable in skirmish.** Campaign-only units: Counterstrike's specials (Volkov, Chitzkoi, the ant missions), RA2's Cosmonaut, and the Navy SEAL in RA2 (it is buildable in Yuri's Revenge). Mark multiplayer-only units (RA1 Tanya, Thief, Mobile Gap Generator, Radar Jammer) in their notes. Counterstrike adds no skirmish units; The Aftermath does, so set `introduced_in: ra1-aftermath`.
- **Units dropped in Yuri's Revenge** (the Soviet Psi-Corps Trooper, and Yuri Prime as Soviet stolen tech) are RA2-only. Say so in their notes, because the researched version is Yuri's Revenge.
- **Unit id examples:** `ra2.iraq.desolator`, `ra2.shared.engineer`, `ra1.shared.rifle-infantry`, `ra2.yuri.lasher-tank`.
- **Veterancy (elite) is not a tier.** It is a passive upgrade earned in battle.

### Red Alert 1 (`ra1`)

- **Armour class** (the armour-class steps).
  - Step 1 reads the armour type through the armour_map: the Armor key in the reference copy of the rules (none when the key is missing), which the infobox `armortype` repeats; where the two differ, the copy wins (the Field Mechanic). The code's types are none, wood, light, heavy and concrete (CONST.CPP); wood and concrete are building armour. The UnitBox `usearmor` field is the wiki's lore, and the manual's Armor word is a grade, not the game's type: neither decides.
  - RA1's types sort units by the warheads that hurt them (DEFINES.H), so heavy covers boats, helicopters and trucks as well as tanks. Step a heavy unit down only when this closed list names it. It goes by our class descriptions and by health against the two anchor tanks:
    - to medium: the Light Tank, APC, both minelayers and the M.A.D. Tank (ground vehicles with less health than the Medium and Heavy Tanks);
    - to light: the Longbow and the Hind (helicopters), and the Gunboat and the naval Transport (boats with less health than the two anchor tanks).
    - Every other heavy unit stays heavy: the Medium, Heavy and Mammoth Tanks, the Ore Truck, the Destroyer and the Cruiser.
  - Where `armortype` is blank:
    - Anti-tank Minelayer: it shares one internal type (MNLY, UDATA.CPP) with the Anti-personnel minelayer, so take that page's heavy; the list then makes it medium.
    - Transport Helicopter (page Chinook (Red Alert 1)): the infobox is empty apart from lore. The reference copy of Rules.ini gives its armour type (light), health, speed and sight in its [TRAN] entry, and StrategyWiki's Soviet units page gives light armour too. Cite the copy (kind database), or name it in an uncertain entry when the unit already has five sources.
    - Infantry: where the infobox gives N/A or leaves the type blank, take it from the reference copy of the rules: none for the Medic, Thief and Flamethrower Infantry (StrategyWiki agrees) and for the Aftermath's Shock Trooper (its [SHOK] entry in aftrmath.ini).
  - Field Mechanic: the reference copy of aftrmath.ini gives the armour type none, as the Aftermath manual's grade does, while his infobox says light. The copy wins: unarmoured, with an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree that names the infobox.
- **Prices.** The Remastered tooltips show the cheaper USSR price ([patch 740600](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Remastered_Collection_patch_740600)); cost_band.raw is the list price. Each Ore Refinery brings an Ore Truck at no charge, and each Helipad an aircraft (manual); raw is still the unit's own price.
- **Page names.** Call the API with `redirects=1`.
  - Production lists: `Allied barracks (Red Alert 1)` and `Soviet barracks (Red Alert 1)` (there is no `Barracks (Red Alert 1)`), `War factory (Red Alert 1)`, `Naval Yard (Red Alert 1)`, `Submarine Pen (Red Alert 1)`, `Helipad (Red Alert)`, `Airfield (Red Alert 1)` and `Kennel`.
  - No suffix: `Rifle infantry`, `Ore truck`, `Transport`, `Cruiser`, `Hind`, `Yak`, `Thief`, `Radar jammer`, `Mobile gap generator`, `V2 rocket launcher`, `Anti-tank minelayer`, `Anti-personnel minelayer`, `Field mechanic`, `Missile submarine`, `M.A.D. tank`, `Phase transport`.
  - `(Red Alert)`: `Grenadier`, `Ranger`, `Artillery`. `(Aftermath)`: `Chrono tank`, `Shock trooper`, `Demolition truck`. The Transport Helicopter is `Chinook (Red Alert 1)`. The rest take `(Red Alert 1)`.
  - Every unit is listed on https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Template:Red_Alert_1_Allied_Arsenal and https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Template:Red_Alert_1_Soviet_Arsenal, which keep campaign-only units in a group of their own.
- **Reading the UnitBox.**
  - `groundattack` and `airattack` give the damage, then the warhead in brackets: that warhead is the damage_map key. `x2` means two shots.
  - Some figures use a decimal comma (3,5 is 3.5).
  - `landspeed`, `seaspeed` and `airspeed` are the same rules Speed value (TECHNO.CPP): they all repeat the rules Speed; band by the Speed section's method, not by dividing by the Rifle Infantry's 4. The Missile submarine page puts its speed under `landspeed`.
  - Weapon range and sight are both in cells (WEAPON.CPP, TECHNO.CPP), so the off-screen test, which compares the two, works.
- **Band outcomes to expect (not errors).**
  - The rifle's reach of 3 is among the game's shortest, so the Medium, Heavy and Mammoth Tanks' guns come out long.
  - Aircraft list sight 0, so their sight band is short, and the Hind, Yak and MiG guns come out off-screen.
  - Also off-screen: the Artillery, V2, both submarines, the Cruiser, the Destroyer's missiles (by their reach against aircraft) and the Rocket Soldier's rockets.
- **Faction and availability traps.**
  - Skirmish sides, by the infobox: the MRJ is Allied; the Transport Helicopter, Yak and MiG are Soviet (their Allied versions are campaign-only); the APC and the Spy are Allied (their Soviet versions are campaign-only). The Phase Transport is campaign-only.
  - Allied Grenadiers, Flamethrowers, Mammoth Tanks and V2s exist only in Counterstrike's ant missions; ignore them.
  - A side that holds a captured enemy production building can build some of the other side's units (manual; the Spy and Helipad pages; HOUSE.CPP in multiplayer). That does not make a unit shared: record it under its own side and say so in notes.
  - The Badger, the Spy Plane and the Paratroopers are powers, not units. Every unit is bought singly, so leave body.count out.
  - Mark the MCV and the Transport Helicopter as multiplayer-only too (the manual's asterisks).
  - Tesla Tank: `introduced_in: ra1-aftermath`. Its Counterstrike version was campaign-only; say so in notes.
  - Longbow: slug `longbow`. The Remastered Collection calls it Attack Helicopter and the manual Apache Longbow; give both names in notes.
- **Figures.** The C&C Wiki and StrategyWiki disagree on some (Rocket Soldier: speed 4 and sight 3 on the C&C Wiki, 3 and 4 on StrategyWiki). Band every rules figure from the reference copy of the rules file, which gives the Rocket Soldier 3 and 4, and add an uncertain entry with sources_disagree where a wiki disagrees. Tanya's infobox has no land speed; take it from the copy (its [E7] entry).
- **Ore Truck death blast.** The [Ore truck](https://cnc.fandom.com/wiki/Ore_truck) page says its load bursts out and hurts nearby units, but the code does that only when the rules key OreExplosive is on (UNIT.CPP); it is off by default (RULES.CPP), and the reference copy of Rules.ini sets it off too. The copy wins on rules values, so code no death-blast, and add an uncertain entry with sources_disagree naming the page. The copy marks the Ore Truck crewed and it has no passenger seats, so the Crew survivors rule gives bail-out, basis in-game.
- **Sources that would not load.** web.archive.org, and the Red Alert Archive's rules.ini downloads (ra.afraid.org answers only over http, and its download link times out). A reference copy of Rules.ini and aftrmath.ini loads instead, over https, from OpenRA's raclassic repository (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRA/raclassic/master/ref/Rules.ini and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRA/raclassic/master/ref/aftrmath.ini; the game reads Rules.ini, then aftrmath.ini on top). By the lead's call it is a source for every rules value, not only Speed and Tracked: the Crewed and Sensors flags, armour types, weapon reaches, projectiles and their flags (AA, ASW, and ROT, which makes a projectile home), and the warheads' damage table. Cite it as kind database, or name it in an uncertain entry when the unit already has five sources. Where it and a wiki disagree on a rules value, the copy wins, with an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree. The Sensors key is the scanner flag that makes a cloaked enemy beside the unit shimmer (FOOT.CPP); the code already sets it for every soldier and every ship (IDATA.CPP, VDATA.CPP), so the copy's Sensors=yes on the Destroyer, Gunboat and Cruiser gives no detector trait by itself.

### Red Alert 2 with Yuri's Revenge (`ra2`)

- **Armour, step 1 (a named step).** The Flak Track steps down one class, to medium. Its armour type is heavy, which resists almost like light (ModEnc Armor types), but the RA2 manual calls it a light vehicle, its health is about 60% of the Grizzly's (its infobox), and the class list puts half-tracks in medium. Every other vehicle keeps its mapped class on purpose: the IFV, Prism Tank and Mirage Tank stay light, and the Robot Tank stays heavy (its infobox; its page's prose about thin armour is the wiki's own words). Say in armour.notes that step 1 decided.
- **Soldiers' armour.** Read the armour type in the unit's infobox. The Conscript, the soldier rule's yardstick, is flak there and the GI is none, so flak and none soldiers are unarmoured and plate soldiers are light (the map). ModEnc's general line that standard infantry wear none does not override the infobox. Soldiers whose infobox gives no type (Crazy Ivan, Chrono Commando, Psi Commando, Cosmonaut, Slave) follow the soldier rule. The Kirov is light in Red Alert 2 and medium in Yuri's Revenge: use medium.
- **Infobox lines that are not the soldier's own weapon.** Infantry infoboxes also give damage and reach inside a garrison and inside an IFV. Those belong to the garrison mechanic and to the IFV. Band the soldier's own weapon from its plain line.
- **Chrono units do not walk.** The Chrono Legionnaire, Chrono Commando and Chrono Ivan teleport, The wiki says their infobox land speed sets how fast they phase back in, but ModEnc's phase-in rule uses only the jump's distance and game-wide settings, and the rules file calls the Legionnaire's speed a dummy. Band them as the Speed section says: very fast, no speed_raw, with an uncertain entry.
- **Tier.** The YR manual's tech tables (PDF pages 15, 17 and 19) list every factory-built unit with its production building and any extra building, country units included. Cite them, kind official, for tier.basis. They leave out the three stolen-tech commandos and the spawned Hornets, Osprey and Slaves. Where an infobox tier disagrees (the Typhoon's says 2, but the table puts the Attack Sub at the Naval Yard alone), take the band from the tier_map.
  - The Chaos Drone is t1, gate War Factory: the page 19 table puts it in the War Factory row with nothing else needed, and its own page gives tier 1. The Psychic radar page and the official site's profile (newunits-yuri2.jsp) still tie it to the Psychic Radar, so keep an uncertain entry marked sources_disagree that names both.
- **Prices at Yuri's Revenge 1.001.** Where 1.001 changed a price, the infobox gives both; use the 1.001 figure: Siege Chopper 1,100 (was 1,400), Slave Miner 1,750 (was 1,500), Chaos Drone 1,000 (was 800). Tanya's infobox now gives 1,500 for Yuri's Revenge, so the price the Prices note calls missing is there. The Chrono Ivan costs 1,750.
- **Stolen tech.** In Yuri's Revenge the lab decides the unit and any barracks then trains it (YR manual, Gameplay Changes item 16): a Spy in an Allied Battle Lab gives the Chrono Commando, in a Soviet one the Chrono Ivan, in Yuri's the Psi Commando. The wiki's infoboxes put all three under the Allies, the only side with Spies. Use `ra2.allied.chrono-commando`, `ra2.allied.chrono-ivan` and `ra2.allied.psi-commando` with the stolen-tech gate, and name the lab in tier.basis.
- **Tech Secret Lab.** In Yuri's Revenge a captured Secret Lab hands out, at random, a unit or structure from another side's or country's list, sometimes one the player could build already; the list includes the Yuri Clone (YR manual; Tech secret lab page). Ignore it when choosing a unit's faction.
- **Korea has no Harrier.** The Black Eagle replaces it (Harrier and Airforce Command Headquarters pages). Record the Harrier under `allied` and name Korea in its notes, as for the Attack Dog.
- **Exact wiki titles for the API.**
  - Allied: GI, Guardian GI, Infantry Fighting Vehicle, Grizzly Battle Tank, Robot Tank, Battle Fortress, NightHawk transport.
  - Soviet (a title marked * ends in " (Red Alert 2)"): Siege chopper; Kirov airship*; Flak Track; Rhino tank*; Terror Drone*; Psi-Corps trooper, the Yuri unit of Red Alert 2.
  - Yuri: Lasher tank, Gattling tank (Yuri's Revenge), Mastermind (Yuri's Revenge), Floating disc, Initiate (Yuri's Revenge), Yuri clone, Yuri Prime (Yuri's Revenge), Boomer. The Soviet stolen-tech Yuri Prime is Yuri Prime (Red Alert 2).
  - Naval: Typhoon attack submarine.
  - The categories Red Alert 2 infantry, vehicles, aircraft and ships, and their Yuri's Revenge twins, list every other unit.

### Red Alert 3 (`ra3`)

- **Version.** Code base-game units at patch 1.12 (16 July 2009, the final patch) and the eleven Uprising units as Uprising has them (12 March 2009; it stands alone, has AI skirmish but no online play, and its wiki infobox gives version 1.0). Uprising runs on unit data from an older Red Alert 3 version (Tsunami tank page). Where it changes a base-game unit (the Apocalypse's grinder damage, the Naginata's Type-S torpedoes, the Century Bomber's clearance), give the Uprising value in notes. A field rule that counts expansions still counts Uprising: passenger weapons count riders from every expansion.
- **StrategyWiki's kind.** Cite StrategyWiki's Red Alert 3 pages as kind wiki, as the pilot samples in data/units/ra3.json and docs/samples.md do. The RA1 rule (kind guide) rests on gaps of those two RA1 pages only: they give no source and leave out the Aftermath units. Batch R1 checked the split and kept both rules.
- **What to research.** All eleven Uprising units can be built in skirmish, and each appears with its gate on its production building's page: Cryo Legionnaire, Pacifier FAV, FutureTank X-1 and Harbinger Gunship; Desolator Trooper, Mortar Cycle, Grinder and Reaper; Archer Maiden, Steel Ronin and Giga-Fortress.
  - Leave out pages in the wiki's RA3 unit categories that no production page lists: the Chief scientist, Ursa Major, the April Fools Mammoth Tank, the Limousine, the President's Chopper, the Floating fortress, the Radar boat, the Salvage ship and the campaign-only Tesla tank (Red Alert 3).
  - Also leave out protocol call-ins, and the Commander's Challenge rules: the Threat Meter page says the Red Alert button works only in that mode.
  - The Nanocore is only the way the Giga-Fortress is built. The Sky Knights belong on the aircraft carrier, as its drones.
- **Page titles.** Several names redirect, so cite the resolved title: Desolator (Red Alert 3) -> Desolator trooper (Uprising); Grinder (Red Alert 3) -> Grinder (Uprising); Reaper (Red Alert) -> Reaper (Uprising); Future Tank X-1 -> FutureTank X-1; Harbinger Gunship -> Harbinger gunship; Archer Maiden -> Archer maiden; Observation Post -> Tech buildings (Red Alert 3); Crates -> Crate. Profiles are at <resolved title>/Profile; Imperial warrior/Profile and Mortar cycle/Profile redirect to Imperial Warrior/Profile and Mortar Cycle/Profile.
- **Ids.** `ra3.allied.futuretank-x-1`, `ra3.allied.pacifier-fav`, `ra3.soviet.desolator-trooper` (lineage `desolator`, after RA2's `ra2.iraq.desolator`; the Uprising manual and the Barracks (Red Alert 3) page call it Desolator Trooper, though its profile heads it Desolator), `ra3.empire.giga-fortress`, `ra3.empire.archer-maiden`.
- **Tier traps.**
  - **Cryo Legionnaire and Pacifier FAV:** gate Defense Bureau (t3). They need Heightened Clearance and a Defense Bureau, two tech steps where t2 has one, and the Defense Bureau is the way into the top Allied tech (Defense bureau page), so it counts as top-level although it needs only an Airbase and a refinery (Allied construction yard (Red Alert 3) page). Their infoboxes and the Heightened Clearance page give tier 2, while the Boot camp (Red Alert 3) and Armor facility pages list them under tier 3: name this in tier.basis and add an uncertain entry with sources_disagree. The FutureTank X-1 and Harbinger Gunship also need Max Clearance, so they are t3 either way.
  - **Century Bomber:** it needs Max Clearance at 1.12, so it is t3. Uprising lowers this to Heightened Clearance (the Heightened Clearance and Max Clearance pages); put that in notes.
  - **Tanya:** she needs Max Clearance, and from 1.11 also a Defense Bureau. She is t3 either way.
  - **Twinblade:** its infobox says tier 1, but the Airfield needs a Super Reactor (Tier page). Its gate is Super Reactor (t2).
  - **Mortar Cycle:** it is trained at the Barracks but needs a War Factory. Its gate is War Factory (t1).
  - **Tsunami Tank:** it comes from the Mecha Bay (Mecha Bay Upgrade) or the Imperial Docks (Docks Upgrade). Both are t2; record the Mecha Bay gate, the first building its infobox names.
  - **Giga-Fortress:** it needs Docks Breakthrough and a Nanotech Mainframe. Its gate is Docks Breakthrough (t3).
  - **Allied clearance is local** (Tier page): a production building builds tier 2 and tier 3 units only while it is linked to a nearby Command Hub or construction yard that holds the clearance. The band still comes from the gate.
- **Prices.** Several infoboxes give one price per patch (Javelin Soldier, Flak Trooper, Prospector, Assault Destroyer, Multigunner IFV). Take the 1.12 figure for base-game units. Ignore the Mass Production protocol and the Crusher Crane's recycling refund.
- **Sight.**
  - Nearly every infobox gives a shroud clearing range of 500 (some pages call it shroud reveal range; it is the same figure), so nearly every unit is medium. Short: the Allied Engineer and Combat Engineer (100) and the Sputnik (250). The Attack Dog's 750 is exactly 1.5 times the Conscript's, so it is medium by the edge rule.
  - Two unlabelled figures, such as the Imperial Warrior's and Tankbuster's 200/500, follow the Conscript's pattern: the larger one is the shroud figure.
  - A lone figure is a vision figure: compare it with the Conscript's vision (200), as the registry says. The Hydrofoil (325) is long, the Sea-Wing (225) medium and the Terror Drone (100) short; patch 1.11's notes give the first two as vision ranges.
  - Nine pages give no sight figure: Burst Drone, Dolphin, Multigunner IFV, Imperial Engineer, Imperial Ore Collector, Steel Ronin, Striker-VX, War Bear and Yari. Their sensors.basis is proposal.
  - Extra sight from Advanced Aeronautics and Fortified Fleet comes from protocols, so it goes in notes only.
- **Speed.**
  - The infobox's landspeed, seaspeed and airspeed are all game-data values, which the speed_raw rule takes as one scale. Divide the figure for the unit's base mobility by the Conscript's 50: aircraft by airspeed, ships by seaspeed, amphibious ground units by landspeed.
  - Figures in brackets for a boost or a button are not the stock speed: the Grinder's Turbo Charge, King Oni's Bull Rush, the Kirov's Gastroburners, the Imperial Warrior's Banzai charge, and the MiG's and Vindicator's Return to Base. Neither is a figure for a mode, such as the Peacekeeper's shield.
  - The Desolator Trooper's page gives no speed.
- **Damage categories.**
  - The infobox's groundattack and airattack lines give each attack's category in brackets.
  - Categories not in the damage map go to step 5: Magic (the Hammer's Leech Beam, which every armour set takes in full), Cryo (the Cryocopter and Cryo Legionnaire; the cryo family gives cryo), Neutron (the FutureTank X-1's scramblers) and Concussive (the torpedoes of the Akula, Riptide ACV, Yari and Naginata, and the Dolphin's attack). Concussive was taken out of the map so that torpedoes are not coded sonic. Spectrum appears only in armour sets.
  - Attack lines with no category also go to step 5. Among them: Tanya, the Tesla Trooper, the Burst Drone, the Combat Engineer's pistol, the Imperial Warrior's rifle and Banzai charge, Yuriko's Psychokinesis, the Hydrofoil's, Sea-Wing's and Striker-VX's weapons, and the IFV's missiles.
  - **Rocket weapons built for aircraft (step 3).** The map makes the Rocket category armour-piercing, since infantry armour sets take little of it while most tank and aircraft sets take it in full. A Rocket-category weapon whose page gives it several times normal damage against aircraft is built mainly to bring down aircraft: record it as explosive, as Flak maps. Only the Reaper's Katyusha rockets do this.
- **Aircraft as targets.** RA3 does not split its fliers: no infobox limits an anti-air weapon to helicopters or to jets. An airattack line in the infobox shows that the weapon attacks air units, as the can_target rule asks: list low air and high air. Without one, list air only when another source says the weapon attacks aircraft.
- **On death.** Death-blast (basis in-game): the Kirov Airship, the Mecha Tengu in jet form and the Striker-VX in chopper form, whose infoboxes give crash damage over an area, and the Sudden Transport, whose attack line includes a blast when it is destroyed. The Century bomber page says only that the falling bomber hurts what it hits, which death-blast does not count: name it in on_death.notes. Honorable Discharge and Terror Drone Surprise go in notes only (see the game-wide list).
- **Availability.** Tanya, Natasha and Yuriko are limited to one per player (Boot camp (Red Alert 3), Barracks (Red Alert 3) and Instant dojo pages), so their availability is unique.
