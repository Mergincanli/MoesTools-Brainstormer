# Round 1: coder hesitations, gaps and unclear rules

442 hesitations on 65 field paths, 102 gaps, 226 unclear rules, from 12 coder reports (coder-game).

Field paths are normalised: indexes and '*' are dropped, so every weapon's range is under armament.range. A hesitation that names more than one field is listed under each of them.

- [C-sc2] sc2.terran.siege-tank weaknesses: used no link; the air weakness is under countered_by as class:aircraft; wanted a negated link such as 'no weapon hits low-air'. Weakness links must describe parts the unit has, so 'cannot shoot at aircraft', its biggest weakness, cannot be written there.
- [C-sc2] sc2.terran.siege-tank armament/1/traits: used none for it; wanted a trait for coordinated or no-overkill fire. Groups of sieged tanks split their shots so they do not overkill one target. This is RTS automation and may not carry over to a player-aimed version.

## Unclear rules

- [A-battlefield] roles tie-break (anti-X vs assault): 'Generalists are assault' sends multi-purpose gunships, jets and IFVs to a role whose description (soak damage, brawl up front) fits only ground brawlers. The anti-X condition is also unclear: does a real-world flavour line in the game's own UI (the Mi-28's Battlelog 'anti-armor attack helicopter') count as 'the game calls the unit that'? And is the condition 'only if' or 'if'?
- [A-battlefield] availability (limited vs unique): Unique is defined both by count ('one per team') and by kind ('heroes and commandos', min_tier t2). Shooter vehicles capped at one per team by the map layout fit the count but not the kind.
- [A-battlefield] sensors.basis: The enum forces one value even when sight is a proposal and the traits are in-game. The doc says to explain in notes but not which value to pick.
- [A-battlefield] sensors in games without fog of war: The sight band is to be 'judged against the game's other units', but shooters like Battlefield have no vision range. Every unit then becomes a free proposal, which will hurt agreement.
- [A-battlefield] sensor traits from loadout picks: Sensor traits are 'always-on' and have no source or option marker, so optional scanners are either overstated (recorded) or lost (omitted).
- [A-battlefield] introduced_in for series: For a 'series' game with no expansions, the validator rejects any id other than the game id, so there is no field for which title a version comes from. Only sources[].version and prose can say it.
- [A-battlefield] weapon slots in multi-seat vehicles: Primary and secondary are unit-wide, but each seat has its own main weapon. When the default of a loadout group is itself an 'option', the unit has no primary-slot weapon. Should the gunner's main gun be primary or secondary?
- [A-battlefield] option weapons with no default: The gunner missile slot is empty by default (both choices are unlocks). The schema has default:true but doesn't say how to mark a slot that starts empty.
- [A-battlefield] weapons gained by unlock progression: Abilities have a source field (innate, upgrade, loadout) but weapons don't, so an unlocked weapon option can't be told apart from a default one except by the default flag.
- [A-battlefield] guided-missiles family vs delivery rules: The family tie-break says any self-steering missile or rocket is a guided missile. The family allows only lock-on and guided delivery. The guided note says 'a shot that homes by itself is lock-on or the homing trait'. A self-homing projectile with no lock step therefore always breaks a rule.
- [A-battlefield] anti-air weapon trait on guided missiles: The trait is defined as lifting a low-air family to high air. On families that already reach high air (guided missiles, autocannon, flak) it is unclear whether to add it to mark purpose.
- [A-battlefield] naval target type: Naval is research-only, yet every family allows it. For shooters with boats (Battlefield), nothing says whether weapons should list naval.
- [A-battlefield] zonal armour model: The definition is parts-based, but its 'seen in' list includes Battlefield 4 mobility kills, which are not location-based. Coders following the definition and coders following the examples will split.
- [A-battlefield] magazine vs finite-ammo: Neither covers a reserve that refills by itself over time, as in BF4 helicopter weapons.
- [A-battlefield] solo_fallback: No term for a seat that keeps its own weapons while another seat's weapons go idle, the common Battlefield case. The full-control 'seen in' (Battlefield tanks) suggests it, but its text says 'fire everything'.
- [A-battlefield] ability source for unlocked picks: Items unlocked by progression and then fitted into a shared slot could be either loadout or upgrade.
- [A-battlefield] patterns.md vs registry on flares: The step-1 notes table folds flares into jam-sensors (break-lock variant), while the correction adds fire-countermeasures. ECM fits both. A rule on linking several patterns to one ability would help.
- [A-battlefield] size scale for aircraft: Sizes are in soldier widths, with only ground examples. It is not said whether a rotor disc or wingspan counts toward the footprint and hitbox.
- [A-battlefield] speed bands in shooters: Every Battlefield aircraft is very fast against a sprinting soldier, so the band carries no information between vehicles. It is also unclear whether speed_raw should be a top speed or a typical speed, and which model's figure to use for a mirrored archetype.
- [A-battlefield] cooldown sourced from an older title: No rule on whether a mechanic value stated only for an earlier title (BF2 flare recharge) may be used for an archetype led by later titles.
- [A-battlefield] counter and combo link kind: Many link kinds can name the same enemy (role:anti-air, hits:high-air, weapon:flak). No preference order is given, which will lower agreement on these lists.
- [A-coh1] roles tie-break ('the game calls the unit that'): It doesn't say whether a wiki's description counts, or only in-game text such as the role field and tooltips. Here the in-game text says 'Core Infantry' and the wiki says 'anti-infantry unit'.
- [A-coh1] weapon slots for squads: In a squad several men fire different guns on one attack order. primary and secondary assume separate fire buttons. There is no guidance on merging a squad's personal weapons into one entry or listing each one, and no slot for weapons added by a bought upgrade.
- [A-coh1] what counts as an ability (the 'separate button' rule) for RTS-wide commands: Reinforce, retreat, garrison, picking up weapons and recrewing team weapons are buttons or orders that every CoH infantry squad has. The rule makes all of them abilities, yet some have no pattern (retreat) and some are arguably functions of buildings (reinforce).
- [A-coh1] linking written patterns from a new unit: source_units must match the units that link a pattern, and the build step that would compute it does not exist yet. A coder linking a written pattern must either edit the pattern file or leave the link out, and the task gives no instruction either way. I left leech-weapon-on-kill out.
- [A-coh1] overlapping patterns: leech-weapon-on-kill vs recover-wreck; throw-explosive vs infest-vehicle ticking-charge: leech-weapon-on-kill's other_examples include CoH abandoned team weapons, which recover-wreck also covers. patterns.md says a timed charge on a vehicle is infest-vehicle's ticking-charge variant, while throw-explosive's summary names sticky charges.
- [A-coh1] cooldown bands: The bands have gaps (3-5 s, 10-15 s, 30-45 s) and edge values (15 s). Nothing says whether to use the cooldown alone or the effect duration plus cooldown.
- [A-coh1] speed band boundaries: The texts overlap at 1.0 ('slow 0.7 to 1', 'medium 1 to 1.5') and at 1.5. Only the validator settles that 1.0 is medium and 1.5 is medium.
- [A-coh1] ability sources: upgrade vs commander-power: Faction-wide purchased unlocks (CoH global upgrades, doctrine command upgrades) fit both descriptions.
- [A-coh1] sensors.basis: It is unclear whether a sight band inferred from a general statement ('most units see X') counts as in-game or proposal.
- [A-coh1] on_death basis for plain deaths: 'respawn' is a player-version concept. Nothing says whether an RTS unit that simply dies should be coded respawn/in-game or respawn/proposal.
- [A-coh1] can_target meaning: It doesn't say whether it means 'may be ordered to fire at' or 'does meaningful damage to', for example rifles against vehicles. It also doesn't say whether to list target types the family allows but that the source game lacks (low-air in CoH1).
- [A-coh1] armour models for infantry: Cover-based damage reduction, a core CoH and shooter mechanic, has no armour model, and per-member squad health has none either. flat-pool is the forced fallback. It is unclear whether CoH's explicit hit-chance system qualifies as 'evasive'.
- [A-coh1] unit-id slugs in typed links and lineage ids: There is no rule for making slugs of unresearched units (whether to drop 'Squad') and no naming rule for lineage ids.
- [A-coh1] confidence 'two independent established sources': It doesn't say whether two pages of the same wiki count as independent.
- [A-coh1] flavour_affinity: It doesn't say whether a unit should default to its faction's registry flavour or be judged on the design alone. Riflemen fit soviet 'cheap numbers' better than the faction's allied tag.
- [A-coh1] cost_band.raw: There is no guidance on reinforcement costs per man or on global upgrades the unit type depends on. The registry only excludes population and upkeep.
- [A-coh1] weapon traits from a mostly-ability effect: The BAR suppresses a little on its own and much more via Suppression Fire. The term-versus-pattern rule doesn't say how strong the always-on effect must be to earn the trait.
- [A-coh1] engineer role vs point capture: The engineer role says 'builds, captures', but the pattern notes say capturing control points is a game-mode rule. It is unclear whether fast point capture supports engineer.
- [A-ra3] damage_map in data/games.json vs armament[].damage: Unclear whether the map binds the damage field or is a hint. It conflicts with family allowed-lists (Terror Drone claws: Explosive/Sniper versus blades; Imperial Warrior laser: Gun versus laser) and has no entry for some game categories (the Leech Beam's Magic). It also doesn't say what to do when one weapon has different categories per target. The registry note implies mapping for RA3 ('no RA2 map ... choose per weapon') without saying so.
- [A-ra3] Conventions: alternate weapon + toggle ability 'linking the same pattern': It doesn't say which pattern: the generic switch-weapon-mode, or the mechanic of the swapped-in weapon (disable-machine, leech-weapon-on-kill). Coders will split.
- [A-ra3] Weapons vs abilities for infest: The infest-vehicle generator allows only the 'active' kind, but in the source the infest is the attack command, so the conventions make it a weapon. Also, an automatic pop-out after a host kill counts as a 'triggered effect' under the conventions; it is unclear whether it should be an ability.
- [A-ra3] Abilities need a pattern vs 'leave the link out and add a fit_note': abilities[].patterns has minItems 1, so an ability with no fitting pattern (e.g. commander-power protocols such as Terror Drone Surprise) cannot be recorded at all. The instruction to leave the link out works only for weapons.
- [A-ra3] Passenger weapons: 'one entry per distinct result' and 'when': Distinct in the game or in our coded terms? 'when' is a single typed link, so a result shared by several passengers (dog or bear) can't be expressed. Slugs of unresearched passenger units are guesses.
- [A-ra3] armament[].when for acquired weapons: There is no guidance on which typed link describes the donor of a weapon taken on a kill.
- [A-ra3] Sight band for RA3: RA3 lists both a vision range and a shroud-clearing range; the band ('how far it sees through the fog') doesn't say which. Some infoboxes (Terror Drone) give only one figure.
- [A-ra3] Range band for weapons with different ranges by target layer: The IFV missiles and similar AA weapons have one range against ground and twice that against air. Which one decides the band is not said.
- [A-ra3] Ability-kinds tie-break deploy vs toggle: 'Deploy when one of the modes is stationary' is unclear when the mode allows driving but forbids firing on the move (Hammer leech mode after patch 1.11).
- [A-ra3] Roles tie-break with conflicting game labels: 'Anti-X only if the game calls the unit that' doesn't rank the game's labels when they conflict: infobox role, official designation, internal name, StrategyWiki role. The Hammer is called both 'Main battle tank' and 'Anti-Armor Tank'. Also, 'never its mechanic' clashes with infiltrator, defined by acting inside units, which is exactly the infest mechanic.
- [A-ra3] crew.basis and crew.model for uncrewed robots: No basis fits 'no crew at all' (in-game seats? lore? proposal?), and nothing says when a drone class unit should be solo versus remote.
- [A-ra3] on_death.basis for an ordinary death: Unclear whether 'respawn' for a unit that simply dies is in-game or proposal.
- [A-ra3] source kind for wiki copies of official text: The C&C Wiki /Profile subpages reproduce the official site; unclear whether they count as official (allowing high confidence) or wiki.
- [A-ra3] mobility.handling as proposal: Unclear whether coders should keep the mobility type's default_handling unless there is a reason, or design freely. This will be a big source of disagreement.
- [A-ra3] Weaknesses that are absences: Strength and weakness links must match the unit's own parts, so 'cannot hit aircraft' has to be attached to some other part (weapon:adaptive, armour-class...). A 'lacks' or 'cannot hit' link kind would make these consistent.
- [A-ra3] Armour-class examples vs game data: The armour-class list names IFVs under medium and buggies under light. The RA3 IFV is a buggy-like IFV with Light armour in the game data, and there is no RA3 armour_map to settle it.

- [A-ra3] Size boundary tiny/small: 'Tiny: dogs, small drones' vs 'Small: one soldier' gives no rule for robots about one soldier wide that ride in infantry slots.
- [A-ra3] Mirrored-term rule vs drain-health: The Leech Beam is a weapon on a toggle. The mirrored-term rule sends the weapon to the drain family, but drain-health is 'on a key' and the toggle is a key. Unclear which applies.

The family choice then forces damage (artillery forbids armour-piercing) and delivery (lobbed only). One judgement therefore decides three fields, which will lower agreement.

- [A-sc2] armour-classes for RTS units: No rule says whether description and role ('heavy tank', 'main battle tank') or toughness compared with the game's other units decides. - [A-sc2] damage-reduction for SC2: The term cites SC2 armour as its example, so literally every SC2 unit gets it, including units whose armour is tiny or zero. A threshold (for example, armour that matters against the game's typical hit) would make it discriminate.
