# Patterns that are one mechanic under two names: the list for Mustafa

A finder read all 69 patterns and proposed pairs; a judge then opened both files of every pair and gave a verdict. Nothing is merged or renamed: a marked pair only means that no card gets both. Each row names a pattern and the variants the verdict holds for.

## 1. The same mechanic (18 pairs)

A card that held both would have the same button, or the same passive, twice.

| # | One entry | The other entry | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2-01 | deal-percent-damage: blast-spends-user | self-destruct (every variant) | Two once-per-life keys that lock the vehicle behind a loud countdown, bail the player out and spend the vehicle; only killing it first stops the blast, or you leave the ring. Only the damage formula differs (a share of health versus a big flat blast). The M.A.D. Tank's one ability links both patterns. |
| L2-02 | passenger-sets-weapon: wreck-bunker | fortify-on-death: cover-wreck | Both throw the players out hurt and leave the dead hull as hard cover with its own health; cover-wreck says it was written from wreck-bunker and has no source. Note: wreck-bunker next to fortify-on-death's anchor-by-choice (rolled as deploy) would give two clashing death outcomes. |
| L2-03 | mind-control: weapon-puppet | infest-vehicle: latched-control | Both take only the victim's weapon for a few seconds while it keeps moving; shake-off and the controller's death end it. A held link versus a latch is delivery, which the Granularity rule makes a variant; mind-control's own seen_in says latched-control does the same from on board. |
| L2-04 | gather-resources: salvage-wrecks | leech-weapon-on-kill: loot-currency | Both turn wrecks into money and both are answered by denying or destroying the wrecks first. A held tool versus a touch, team money versus own materials, are variant-size. salvage-wrecks' own 'unlike leech-weapon-on-kill' line (leech needs the unit's own kill) is not backed: leech's base counts touching a wreck as a claim. |

| L2-12 | teleport: home-jump | gather-resources: warp-home | Both teleport the unit back to a fixed friendly point to bank cargo or escape, both from Red Alert 2's Chrono Miner; the answer is to catch it on the way out or at the field. warp-home can also be ordered home early, so home-jump adds nothing to a card that has it. |
| L2-15 | rally-allies: shadow-one | join-ally-crew: squad-attach | Both tie the card to one teammate's squad, follow it, and keep it steadier and healing faster while attached; the answer is to kill or separate the buffer and splash the group. | L2-27 | launch-drones: scout-drone | reveal-area (every variant) | The scout drone widens sight, reveals stealthed units or marks enemies over a chosen spot for the team: reveal-area delivered by a drone, and delivery is a variant under the Granularity rule. | L2-33 | bombard-area: planted-beacon | throw-explosive: demolition-charge | Both are planted by hand and run a long countdown the enemy can see; only reaching and disarming them in time stops them, so the planter's team guards them. | X-02 | sacrifice-for-allies: ranged-offering | rally-allies: placed-blessing | As L2-16, but even closer: minions are spent from a carrier onto a distant spot, so no walk to shoot at; what is left is a buff placed on an area at range for a price, which is placed-blessing. |
| X-03 | gather-resources: deploy-processor | deploy-siege-mode: become-structure | Both are the Yuri's Revenge Slave Miner deploying into a refinery that earns for the team and can't move while deployed. Both imply the deploy-anchor trait, so under K-d they would even share one key: one button, its deployed state written twice. |

## 2. Mostly a repeat (18 pairs)

The two share most of the action; each has a small part of its own, but a card holding both gets mostly the same tool twice. The judge would still keep them off one card.

| # | One entry | The other entry | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2-11 | dash: untouchable-dash | become-invulnerable: phase-out | Two keys that make the unit untouchable while it slips through or away; the answers are to wait it out, silence it before it starts and punish the end. The dash is an instant straight burst that slows and roots cut short; phase-out lasts a moment with full steering, may hide the unit, and blocks firing and objectives. |
| L2-18 | capture-structure: clamp-a-device | disable-machine: clamp-on-stunner | Both stick a device on an enemy thing that stops it until the device is knocked off; the answer is to repair it off or kill the sapper. Team Fortress 2 runs both as one sapper. The split is by target and effect (structures: lasting shutdown with damage; machines: short stun, then recharge); on a turret the two buttons are identical. |
| L2-19 | passenger-sets-weapon: anchor-to-fire | fortify-on-death: anchor-by-choice | Both put the carrier on a key into a static bunker so riders can fire; the answers are to catch it moving, flank it and out-range the riders. Both split Kane's Wrath's Reckoner. anchor-by-choice adds fortify-on-death's second life and is one way; anchor-to-fire may pack up. If anchor-by-choice rolls 'triggered', the pair rebuilds the Reckoner with no repeat; as two deploy keys it repeats. |
| L2-21 | burrow (every variant) | self-destruct: buried-trap | Both lie buried on a route and fire automatically on the first enemy near; the answers are detection, scans and avoiding the obvious paths. The payoff differs: tripwire-ambush surfaces to fight on, buried-trap blows up and is spent. Both cite StarCraft II banelings buried as mines. |
| L2-22 | lay-mines: be-the-mine | burrow (every variant) | Both bury the card on a route and strike the first enemy that comes near; the answers are a detector, and shooting it when it shows itself. be-the-mine fires one heavy shot from below and reloads in place; tripwire-ambush surfaces. Two deploy keys that dig the unit in. |
| L2-23 | infest-vehicle: mine-latch | lay-mines: snare-trap | Both plant one or two hidden traps that slow the first enemy over them and ping the owner; the answers are to detect, sweep or take another route. mine-latch works on vehicles only and leaves a lasting slow or tracker tag that must be shaken or repaired off; the snare holds any enemy for a moment. |
| L2-25 | mind-control: hack-machine | capture-structure: take-over | Both turn an enemy turret or device to your side after a warned hack; the answers are to interrupt the hack, kill the hacker and retake it. hack-machine works from range on crewless machines (drones and robots too; on a player-driven one only the weapon, briefly); take-over is a contact hold on structures and buildings. On turrets and devices the two buttons do the same thing. |
| L2-26 | build-linked-exits: recall-to-carrier | teleport: move-teammates | Both bring teammates out at your position on a key; the answers are to punish the arrivals and kill the caller. recall-to-carrier needs a shelter first, a safe waiting place that dies with everyone inside; move-teammates pulls from an area or swaps. The Thunderhead's single Deploy/Recall ability links build-linked-exits and teleport. |
| L2-28 | shroud-area: thrown-blackout | lay-smoke: lobbed-smoke | Both throw a device that hides allies in an area for several seconds; the answers are to walk in, fire area weapons blind or wait it out. The blackout is one-way (allies see out, enemies outside see nothing); smoke blocks sight both ways and can be thrown on enemy guns to blind them. A card with both holds two thrown concealment clouds. |
| L2-29 | gather-resources: worker-crew | reinforce-squad: respawning-followers | worker-crew's own text says lost workers come back as in reinforce-squad's respawning-followers, so a card with both writes the same respawn twice; the answers are to kill the soft followers, then the master. Own parts: the workers earn income; respawning-followers may be an escort. |
| L2-30 | self-destruct: disguised-bomb | disguise (every variant) | disguised-bomb is a self-destruct carrier that already looks like an ordinary vehicle; copy-target adds a key to choose whose look. | L2-32 | throw-explosive: remote-charge | lay-mines: command-trigger | Both lay explosives to trap a route and blow them by hand when the right target comes. | L2-34 | build-structure: deploy-into-structure | deploy-siege-mode: become-structure | Both are a deploy key that unfolds the vehicle into a team building; the answers are to catch it on the way and then hit the building. deploy-into-structure is one way (a build-structure building such as a forward base); become-structure packs up again (a small job building such as a refinery). | X-05 | build-structure: deploy-into-structure | produce-unit-in-field: unfold-into-factory | The third side of the L2-34 and L3-20 cluster: two deploy keys that turn the vehicle into a team building (one way versus packs up). Treat the cluster as one rule. |

## 3. Overlap, but a real second tool (9 pairs): no marking proposed

| # | One entry | The other entry | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2-09 | heal-from-debuff: borrowed-time | become-invulnerable: cant-die | Both open a short window in which the protected unit can't be killed. borrowed-time is self-only (a key, or automatic at low health) and every hit heals it, so the answer is to hold fire; cant-die covers allies in a thrown device's field or one chosen ally, hits still land down to a floor, so the answer is to keep firing, shoot the device and burst when it ends. One saves you, the other your team. |
| L2-13 | teleport: beacon-jump | rewind-self: set-anchor | Both drop a marker and jump to it later, and enemies can camp it. The beacon is thrown ahead (engage or flank, position only, and a damaged beacon kills the jumper); the anchor is set where you stand and also restores the health you had, which is rewind-self's core. set-anchor's own seen_in says Sombra's placed Translocator saved position only. Together: an engage and escape kit, like Tracer's. |
| L2-14 | earn-veterancy: carried-rank | join-ally-crew: rank-courier | One Generals Pilot rule told from two cards: on the vehicle's card the pilot keeps its own rank for its next vehicle (passive); on the rider's card a courier carries a lost vehicle's ranks on foot and gives them to a teammate's hull, once, on a key. Same answer (catch the pilot or courier on foot). |
| L2-17 | build-structure: builder-drone | launch-drones: support-drone | Both send a fragile helper drone off to repair and help build, answered by shooting or EMPing the drone or killing the owner. builder-drone is tied to the build menu (build, repair, capture: Supreme Commander's worker drone); support-drone heals, shields or repairs a teammate. A card with both gets two jobs that share only repair. |
| L2-20 | transform: shell-and-pilot | drop-in-from-above: call-down-machine | Both lose the machine, fight on as a pilot and get a machine back; the answers are to kill the pilot before it returns and meet the arrival. But shell-and-pilot is a triggered death into a pilot form, and call-down-machine is the key that brings a fresh machine down: together they rebuild D.Va, whose new mech arrives by a call-down, rather than one button twice. |
| L2-24 | execute-target: health-threshold | freeze-and-shatter: shatter-threshold | Both kill outright below a health line, and staying above it is the answer. health-threshold is your own finishing strike on one target, ready again after a kill; shatter-threshold marks targets so they can't heal and anyone's damage below the line shatters them, which takes away the usual answer (heal back above the line). |
| L2-31 | leave-hazard-zone: slow-field | dampen-missile-fire: slow-all-attacks | Both slow the attacks of enemies in an area, and the answer is to leave the field. slow-field is a thrown or placed timed zone that also slows movement, a tool to catch enemies; slow-all-attacks is a passive aura around a standing unit that slows attack rate only, a tool to protect allies near it. |
| L2-36 | repair-allies: revive | host-spawn-point: call-in-the-fallen | Both bring dead teammates back near the front without the trip from base. A revive needs the body and a channel (answer: interrupt it, camp the body); call-in-the-fallen brings them down beside you from a shared pool on a key (answer: jam it, meet the arrival). |
| L2-37 | deploy-turret: leave-machine-as-turret | drop-in-from-above: earned-war-machine | Both leave a machine that fights by itself while its pilot is out (Titanfall's Auto-Titan). leave-machine-as-turret is a toggle on the suit you started in; earned-war-machine is a meter-fed ultimate that calls a second, heavy machine down from above. The shared part is the machine's behaviour, not a button. |

## 4. Rejected: different mechanics (1)

| # | One entry | The other entry | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2-07 | convert-on-kill: death-curse | execute-target: spawn-from-victim | The execute strike kills its prey outright and the minions are a by-product; the death curse kills nothing, it marks one soldier so that anyone's kill of it raises a minion. Answers differ: shoot the fragile hunter on its approach and stay together, versus pull back before dying while cursed. Only the minion clean-up (splash) is shared; convert-on-kill draws the line itself, and its OQ[5] is a filing question. |

## 5. Already kept apart by the patterns themselves (3)

| # | One entry | The other entry | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1-01 | stack-to-overload (every variant) | freeze-and-shatter (every variant) | Hits fill a meter on the victim, a full meter leaves it helpless for a moment, a heavy hit cashes it in; the victim breaks contact early or shakes off with F, its team hunts the builder or freezer. stack-to-overload calls itself a variant of freeze-and-shatter; vehicles only, the overloaded vehicle still crawling and firing, and pips on ordinary weapons are variant-size. affinity-bonus and brittle-chill are nearly word for word; outright-execution is not the mirror of shatter-threshold (a finisher kill versus a health-line execute). |

The helper drone takes orders on a key, does chores, revives its owner once and is not targeted by enemies (answer: kill the owner); the bought add-on follows by itself, can be shot down (answer: shoot it) and is bought again. |
